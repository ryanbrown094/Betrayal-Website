A night of The Betrayal

Real screenshots from a game, in the version you choose.

Everyone plays on their own phone, and the host plays too. The TV, if you use one, puts on the show. Here is a whole evening, from opening the Table to the last reveal. (Some surprises are left out on purpose: you’ll meet them on the night.)

Before anyone arrives

You open your Table on your own phone with your booking code and your name. Then you choose how the Forsworn kill (poison or murder), whether Sanctuary is secret or public, the prize, and whether dinner is served in courses. Guests can join from this moment.

Everyone joins

Guests scan the code on the TV, or go to thebetrayal.co.uk/join and type the Table code and their name. No app, no accounts.

The Oath

Every phone asks its player, privately, whether they’d like to be Forsworn. The game deals the roles with the answers in mind, though nobody is promised what they asked for.

Your secret role

Each player holds their phone close and taps to see their role. The Forsworn learn who the other Forsworn are. Then a tap hides it again.

A new day

Each round starts at the table. The host’s phone shows one button, the next thing in the evening’s plan, and the game plans the rest.

The Trials

Party games played on the phones: quizzes, guesses, words, memory, charades, rock paper scissors and more. Each one fills the Purse, most give the winner Sanctuary for the night, and the Forsworn do best by quietly losing.

More of the fifteen Trials

Breaks, and the pause

The host can call a short break between parts, and the game serves dinner in courses if you’ve switched it on. If the doorbell goes, the host can pause: every clock stops until they carry on.

The Reckoning

Talk, accuse and defend on a timer, then everyone votes in secret on their phone. The vote closes when everyone’s in. The exiled player’s role is shown, and they can leave the table one last line.

Night

Heads down. Everyone taps a name on their phone so nobody can tell who is really choosing. Only the Forsworn’s choices count.

Dawn

The victim finds out first, in secret, on their own phone. Then the host reveals the night to everyone.

The end

When five or fewer remain, exiles are no longer revealed. Once the survivors all trust each other, the masks come off one by one, and the prize is won.

Classic: the old-fashioned way

Prefer phones away? In Classic, one phone runs the game: the Keeper reads every line and doesn’t play, guests wear blindfolds at night and chalk their votes, and the TV puts on the show. The rest of this page is a Classic night.

Before the guests arrive

The Keeper sets the game up on their phone: five questions (who’s playing, how long, murder or poison, the prize, who keeps), and the rest is planned from the answers. Every choice is explained, and any of it can be changed before the game starts. A first game keeps the twists off, so everyone learns the game itself.
