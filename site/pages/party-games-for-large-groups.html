Party games for large groups of adults

Most party games are built for four to six people. Past eight, half the room ends up waiting for a turn. The Betrayal is a party game made for large groups of 8 to 24 adults in one room. Everyone plays at once, on their own phone, from start to finish.

What makes a good game for a big group

  • Everyone plays at the same time. Nobody should spend twenty minutes watching someone else’s turn.
  • It’s easy to join. No rulebook to read in advance, no app to download, no accounts. In The Betrayal, guests scan a QR code or go to thebetrayal.co.uk/join and type a code and their name.
  • Nobody has to run it. A game that needs a quizmaster or a narrator costs you a guest. The Betrayal plans and runs itself, so nobody sits out.
  • It gets people talking to each other, not just to a screen. The best large-group games give the room something to argue about.
  • It’s the right length. A big group takes a while to settle, so a game that’s over in twenty minutes leaves you looking for the next one. A game that runs past midnight loses people. For a big group, The Betrayal takes an hour or two.

How The Betrayal works

At the start, every phone asks its player a private question: would you like to be Forsworn? A few players become the Forsworn, the secret traitors, and they know who each other are. Everyone else is Loyal, and they have one evening to find the Forsworn.

Each round, the group plays a Trial, which is a party game on the phones. It might be a quiz, charades where only the actor sees the word, a word game, a guessing game or rock, paper, scissors. Trials fill a shared prize pot called the Purse. The winner of a Trial usually gets Sanctuary, which keeps them safe from the Forsworn that night. Then comes the Reckoning. Everyone argues for a few minutes about who might be Forsworn, then votes in secret on their phone. The player with the most votes is exiled, which means voted out. At night the Forsworn secretly choose someone to poison. Everyone else taps their phone too, so nobody can tell who is choosing. At the end comes the Unmasking, when every role is shown, then the prize and up to six awards. The rules have the whole game, and the tour shows it screen by screen.

How the size of the group changes the game

The Betrayal takes 8 to 24 players and is at its best with 8 to 16. It plans its own rounds for your group, so that the final round starts with five or fewer players left, or four in a game of 8. Which parties it suits

Christmas parties and office dos

An office Christmas party game has to work for people who don’t all know each other, in a room that’s often too big for a board game. The Betrayal needs only a phone each, and a big screen or projector if the venue has one. Colleagues discover a talent for lying that nobody suspected. Keep it friendly by choosing a prize everyone would like to win. People who are out become Ghosts, so they can still guess who goes at each vote and play many of the Trials.

All through December, the quiz asks fifteen Christmas questions, from the Twelve Days to Die Hard. Most Likely To goes festive too, with prompts like who’ll eat all the mince pies and who’s on the naughty list.

Birthday parties for adults

For a birthday with a houseful of friends, it gives the evening a shape: drinks, the Oath, a few rounds and a reveal to finish. Make the birthday guest the host. They open the Table on their phone and still get a secret role like everyone else.

Hen and stag parties

A hen or stag do mixes friends from different parts of someone’s life. A game of secret roles and accusations gets them talking fast. The awards at the end, such as The Grand Deceiver and The Sharpest Eye, give everyone a story for the next day.

Games nights

If your games night has outgrown the board games on the shelf, The Betrayal is the one to bring out when twelve people turn up. Everyone plays in their browser, so there’s nothing to set up beyond sharing the code.

Dinner parties

Play it before or after you eat. On the phones, a game for ten takes about 45 minutes. For a slower evening built around the meal, play Classic. A Keeper runs the game from one phone, and dinner in courses fits between the rounds.

An alternative to a murder mystery party

A murder mystery party is a lot of work for the host. There are characters to cast weeks ahead, costumes, booklets to read, a script to follow, and a solution that one person already knows. The Betrayal gives you the same suspicion and the same body count at the table, with none of the homework:

  • No scripts and no characters. Everyone plays themselves, and the lies are their own.
  • Nobody knows the answer in advance, including the host. The Forsworn are chosen at the table, on the night.
  • Any number from 8 to 24. There’s no fixed cast, so a late arrival or a cancellation doesn’t spoil anything.
  • You can play it again. The roles change every game, and the Trials draw on thousands of questions and prompts, so nothing repeats in a game.
  • It’s a game, with a winner. The Loyal win if they find every Forsworn. The Forsworn win if one of them survives.

Nobody is murdered, exactly. The Forsworn poison someone each night, and the victim finds out in secret on their phone before telling everyone at dawn.

What you need

  • 8 to 24 people, and between 45 minutes and two hours. It takes about 45 minutes for 8 to 10 people, and up to about two hours for 24.
  • A phone each, with a browser. There’s no app to download and no account to make.
  • A prize. Unless you choose something else, it’s chocolate coins, one for every 100 points the winners take from the Purse.
  • A TV if you have one, with a laptop connected or casting to it. It puts the questions, votes and reveals on the big screen, with music, but it’s optional.
