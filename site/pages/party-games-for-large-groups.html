Party games for large groups of adults

Most party games are built for four to six people. Past eight, half the room ends up waiting for a turn. The Betrayal is a party game made for 8 to 24 adults in one room, where everyone plays at once, on their own phone, for the whole evening.

What makes a good game for a big group

  • Everyone plays at the same time. Nobody should spend twenty minutes watching someone else’s turn.
  • It’s easy to join. No rulebook to read in advance, no app to download, no accounts. In The Betrayal, guests scan a QR code or go to thebetrayal.co.uk/join and type a code and their name.
  • Nobody has to run it. A game that needs a quizmaster or a narrator costs you a guest. The Betrayal plans and runs itself, so nobody sits out.
  • It gets people talking to each other, not just to a screen. The best large-group games give the room something to argue about.
  • It lasts the evening. A big group takes a while to settle. A game that’s over in twenty minutes leaves you looking for the next one.

How The Betrayal works

At the start, every phone asks its player a private question: would you like to be Forsworn? A few players become the Forsworn, who know each other. Everyone else is Loyal, and has one evening to find them.

Each round the group plays a Trial, a party game on the phones: a quiz, charades where only the actor sees the word, a word game, a guessing game, rock-paper-scissors. Trials fill a shared Purse and win Sanctuary from the night. Then comes the Reckoning: a few minutes of argument, a secret vote on every phone, and someone is exiled. At night the Forsworn poison someone, while everyone else taps their phone too so nobody can tell who is choosing. It ends with the Unmasking, the prize and up to six awards. The rules have the whole game, and the tour shows it screen by screen.

How the size of the group changes the game

The Betrayal takes 8 to 24 players and is at its best with 8 to 16. It plans its own rounds for your group, so the final starts with five or fewer players left. Bigger groups get more Forsworn, more exiles and more poisonings, and a longer night. The numbers drop as the table shrinks:

Which parties it suits

Christmas parties and office dos

An office Christmas party game has to work for people who don’t all know each other, in a room that’s often too big for a board game. The Betrayal needs only a phone each, and a big screen or projector if the venue has one. Colleagues discover a talent for lying that nobody suspected. Keep it friendly by choosing a prize everyone would like to win, and let the people who are out keep calling the votes as Ghosts.

Birthday parties for adults

For a birthday with a houseful of friends, it gives the evening a shape: drinks, the Oath, a few rounds, dinner if you want it, and a reveal to finish. Make the birthday guest the host: they open the Table on their phone and still get a secret role like everyone else.

Hen and stag parties

A hen or stag do mixes friends from different parts of someone’s life. A game of secret roles and accusations gets them talking fast, and the awards at the end (The Grand Deceiver, The Sharpest Eye) give everyone a story for the next day.

Games nights

If your games night has outgrown the board games on the shelf, The Betrayal is the one to bring out when twelve people turn up. Everyone plays in their browser, so there’s nothing to set up beyond sharing the code.

Dinner parties

Dinner is optional. If you’re eating together, switch on dinner in courses and the game stops for a starter, the main course and dessert, each in its place between the rounds.

An alternative to a murder mystery party

A murder mystery party is a lot of work for the host: characters to cast weeks ahead, costumes, booklets to read, a script to follow, and a solution that one person already knows. The Betrayal gives you the same suspicion and the same body count at the table, with none of the homework:

  • No scripts and no characters. Everyone plays themselves, and the lies are their own.
  • Nobody knows the answer in advance, including the host. The Forsworn are chosen at the table, on the night.
  • Any number from 8 to 24. There’s no fixed cast, so a late arrival or a cancellation doesn’t spoil anything.
  • You can play it again. The roles change every game, and the Trials draw on thousands of questions and prompts, so nothing repeats in a game.
  • It’s a game, with a winner. The Loyal win if they find every Forsworn; the Forsworn win if one of them survives.

Nobody is murdered, exactly: the Forsworn poison someone each night, and the victim finds out in secret on their phone before announcing it at dawn.

What you need

  • 8 to 24 people, and an evening: about 3 to 3½ hours for 8 to 12.
  • A phone each, with a browser. There’s no app to download and no account to make.
  • A prize. Chocolate coins, unless you choose something else: one coin for every 100 points the winners take from the Purse.
  • A TV if you have one, with a laptop connected or casting to it. It puts the questions, votes and reveals on the big screen, with music, but it’s optional.
