How an evening goes

  1. You open your TableYour booking code, your name, murder or poison, and the prize. That’s the setup.
  2. Everyone scans inThe code on the TV, or thebetrayal.co.uk/join. No app, no accounts, just a name.
  3. The OathEach phone asks, privately: would you like to be Forsworn? Then the roles arrive, one screen at a time.
  4. The TrialsQuizzes and games on the phones, timed and scored for you. They fill the Purse. The Forsworn lose them on purpose, quietly.
  5. The ReckoningTalk, accuse, defend, then vote in secret. Someone is exiled, and the table learns what they were.
  6. NightHeads down. Everyone taps a name so nobody can tell who’s choosing. By dawn, someone is dead.
  7. The masks come offFive left, then fewer. When the table trusts everyone who remains, every role is shown and the prize is won.

Before you ask

Does everyone need a phone?

Yes: everyone plays on their own, in the browser, with nothing to install. If your group would rather put phones away, play Classic: only the Keeper uses one, guests wear blindfolds at night and chalk their votes. You choose when you open your Table.

What does booking a Table get me?

One Table for one party (£10). It stays open for 48 hours from when you first open it, so you can set up in the afternoon and play into the small hours.

What’s a Regular?

A Regular pays £20 a month and gets one booking code that starts a new game whenever you like — one game at a time, each open for 48 hours. It renews monthly until you cancel, which you can do any time with “Manage subscription”.

Does the host get to play?

Yes. The host’s phone also says when to begin the next part and when to take a break, but it never shows anyone’s role. In Classic, the game’s Keeper runs it like a presenter, and knows who the Forsworn are from the start, so the Keeper doesn’t play. The host can still play: ask a friend to keep. You plan the game on your phone, then hand it over before anyone sits down, and your friend presses Start, so you never see the roles.

How long does it take?

About 3 to 3½ hours for 8 to 12 players; stopping for dinner adds half an hour to an hour. Bigger tables need more rounds: about 5 hours for 16, and 6 or more for 20 and up. In Classic there’s also a Short game (one Trial a round, no breaks) of about 2 hours for 8 to 10 players, and the Keeper’s setup shows an estimate for your table.

Why “The Betrayal”?

It starts with an oath. Every guest swears to be loyal to the table, and a few of them don’t mean it: they are the Forsworn. Each night a candle goes out, and the Loyal have until the last one to find out who betrayed them.

Murder or poison?

Your choice, when you open your Table. The victim finds out in secret on their phone during the night. Murdered players leave the table quietly at dawn; poisoned players announce it, get their last words, then leave. (In Classic, the Keeper taps them while everyone is blindfolded.)

What if the Wi-Fi drops?

Every phone reconnects by itself and picks up where it was: the game lives on our server, not on anyone’s phone. If someone’s battery dies, they rejoin on another phone and the host lets them back in. Nothing is lost.

Who wins the Purse?

The Loyal, if they win, take the Purse they built. The Forsworn, if they win, take what it fell short of its target, so every point they quietly sabotage is theirs. More in the rules.
