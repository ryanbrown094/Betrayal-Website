How to play

These are the rules of The Betrayal, a party game for 8 to 24 players. They cover everything that happens in a game, from the first oath to the last betrayal. You don’t need to learn any of it before you play, because every phone tells its player what to do and when. But if you like to know what you’re buying, here is the whole game.

This page describes the game as most people play it, with everyone on their own phone. For a longer, more immersive evening, there’s Classic, with a Keeper, blindfolds and chalk.

The idea

A few of your guests are secretly the Forsworn. They are the traitors, and they know who each other are. Everyone else is one of the Loyal, and the Loyal don’t know who anyone is. Every night the Forsworn poison someone, which puts that player out of the game. Every day, everyone votes to throw someone out. The Loyal win if they vote out every Forsworn before the end. The Forsworn win if even one of them is still in the game when it finishes.

In between, you play party games called Trials. They fill a shared prize pot, and they can win you protection from the poison for a night. You argue, you make alliances, and you lie to people you’ve known for years. A game lasts from about 45 minutes to two hours, depending on how many play, so have drinks and snacks ready.

Joining

The host opens the Table, which is your game for the evening, by entering their booking code and their name. Everyone else scans the QR code on the TV, or goes to thebetrayal.co.uk/join and types the six-character Table code and their name. There’s nothing to install and no account to make. The TV is optional. Without one, the host reads the Table code out from their phone. When everyone has joined, the host taps Start. The game needs at least 8 players.

Who’s who

The Loyal are most of the players. They don’t know anyone else’s role. Their job is to work out who the Forsworn are and vote them out. They have nothing to go on but what people say, how they vote and their gut feeling.

The Forsworn are about one guest in four or five. Their phones tell them who the other Forsworn are. Each night they secretly choose someone to poison. By day they pretend to be Loyal, steer the vote towards other players, and quietly do badly in the Trials.

The host has a secret role like everyone else and never sees anyone else’s. Their phone also moves the evening on. It shows a button for the next part of the game, and the host taps it when everyone is ready. Before each Trial, the host taps to start it once everyone has heard the rules. The host can also call a break, open the vote early or add a minute to a clock. The game itself chooses the Trials and plans the rounds.

Sometimes a game can’t carry on, for example because a phone dies and nobody remembers who was Forsworn. As a last resort, the host’s tools can then show the roles. The phone asks three times first, and everyone is told at the end that the host looked.

The night begins with the Oath. Every phone asks its player, in secret, whether they would like to be Forsworn. They can say yes, say they’d rather be Loyal, or say either way. The game uses the answers to choose the roles, and it picks volunteers first, but nobody is promised what they asked for. Then each player holds their phone close and looks at their role.

Nobody is told how many Forsworn there are. You only find out as Forsworn are voted out and their roles are shown.

A round

The game is played in rounds. It plans how many from the number of players, so that the final round starts with five or fewer players left. With 8 players it starts with four or fewer, which gives them a second night. Each round goes like this:

  1. A Trial. A Trial is a party game played on the phones, such as a quiz, a guessing game or charades. They are all listed below. Each question closes by itself when everyone has answered or the clock runs out. Every Trial adds to the Purse, the shared prize pot. Most Trials also give Sanctuary to the winner, which keeps them safe from the poison that night. The evening opens with Most Likely To, as an icebreaker.
  2. The Reckoning. This is the debate and the vote. First, everyone argues about who might be Forsworn while a timer runs. The timer gives thirty seconds for each player still in, up to four minutes. Then everyone votes in secret on their phone. The vote closes when everyone has voted, and once all but one have voted, the last player has thirty seconds. The player with the most votes is exiled, which means voted out. If there’s a tie, everyone votes again between the tied players. If that ties too, the game picks one of them at random. While more than sixteen players are left, two players are exiled each time.
  3. The reveal. Everyone sees whether the exiled player was Forsworn or Loyal. On the way out, the exiled player can type one last line, which the TV shows.
  4. The night. Everyone looks down at their phone. The Forsworn secretly choose someone to poison, and everyone else taps a name too, so nobody can tell who is really choosing. While twelve or more players are left, the Forsworn choose two people. While twenty or more are left, they choose three. All the Forsworn must choose the same people, so the night waits until they agree. If the clock runs out first, the players chosen most are poisoned. Once the Forsworn agree, the last player still to tap has thirty seconds. A player holding Sanctuary can’t be poisoned. The Forsworn also have a secret chat of their own, under Your role on their phones, which they can use at any time.
  5. Dawn. This is the morning. The poisoned player finds out first, in secret, on their phone. Then the host shows everyone what happened in the night. The victim was poisoned, which means they are out of the game. They stand up, tell everyone, and get thirty seconds of last words before they leave the table. If everyone once voted them most likely to do something, the TV mentions that too.

The host can call a short break whenever they like. They can also pause the game, which stops every clock, for the doorbell or a top-up of drinks. If someone is taken ill, the host can take them out of the game without anyone learning their role.

The dead

Nobody goes home when they’re out. Players who are exiled or poisoned become Ghosts. They stay at the party and watch the game on their phones. Ghosts can’t vote. But at every Reckoning they can say who they think should go, and everyone is told which Ghosts guessed right. In many Trials the Ghosts play on for a score of their own. It never touches the Purse, the Trial’s winner or Sanctuary, and the best Ghost of the night can win an award. In The Statue, the Ghosts do their worst to make the living laugh.

Sanctuary

Sanctuary keeps its holder safe from the poison for one night. It’s the prize in most Trials. At most a quarter of the players can hold it at the same time. Before the game starts, the host chooses whether Sanctuary is public or secret. If it’s public, everyone is told who won it. If it’s secret, only the winner knows they’re safe. A Forsworn who wins a secret Sanctuary gets nothing, and nobody else knows.

Twists

Twists are surprises that change the game for a moment. Each one comes once in the evening, and the phones explain it when it happens. The host can switch them all off before the start, for a simpler first game.

  • The Casting Vote comes about halfway through. The winner of that Trial gets it instead of Sanctuary, and nobody else knows who has it. It’s a secret tie-breaker. If a Reckoning ties and its holder voted for one of the tied players, their vote decides it.
  • In the Temptation, everyone secretly chooses PURSE or SELF. If exactly one person chooses SELF, they get Sanctuary and the Purse grows. If nobody chooses SELF, the Purse grows by double. If two or more choose SELF, nobody gets Sanctuary and the Purse shrinks.
  • The Crown comes in the last round before the final, and everyone is told who wins it. It gives its holder Sanctuary that night. Also, once, at a Reckoning, the holder can lay it down to be safe from that vote.
  • The Confessor comes in the final. The winner of that Trial is told, in secret, the name of one Forsworn who is still playing.

The Purse

The Purse is the shared prize pot. Every Trial adds to it, up to a target, and the better everyone plays, the more goes in. The Trial in the final plays for everything the Purse is still missing, so a perfect final fills it. Some Trials are knockouts, where players drop out until one is left: Rock Paper Scissors Royale, One Leg and The Statue. They have a winner but no score for the whole group, so they add half their share.

The winners take the Purse at the end. If the Loyal win, they take what they built. If the Forsworn win, they take what it fell short of the target. So every point the Forsworn quietly lose is a point for them. Each wrong answer from a Forsworn takes points out of the Purse. Everyone hears how many wrong answers there were, but never whose. This doesn’t apply to the Rapid Quiz’s brutal questions, which beat most people. A Loyal player’s wrong answers cost nothing, so a shrinking Purse is a clue.

There is also a real prize, chosen by the host. Make it something worth lying to your friends for. If the host doesn’t choose one, it’s chocolate coins, one for every 100 points the winners take from the Purse. The host can also set better prizes for a fuller Purse. If the Forsworn win, it works the other way round, so an emptier Purse wins them a better prize. The players know that the Purse decides the prize, but not what the prize will be.

The end game

The last round is the final. It starts when five or fewer players are left, or four or fewer in a game of 8. There is no night in the final.

  1. A last Trial. It plays for everything the Purse is still missing, and perhaps something more. There’s no Sanctuary in the final, because there is no night left to survive.
  2. The Reckoning, without reveals. Everyone votes a player out, but this time their role is not shown.
  3. Trust or Doubt. When four or fewer are left, each player still in makes a secret choice on their phone. They choose TRUST if they believe every Forsworn is gone, or DOUBT if not. Once all but one have chosen, the last player has thirty seconds, and no answer counts as DOUBT. The game only ends if everyone chooses TRUST. Otherwise it’s back to the Reckoning for another exile, then another Trust or Doubt. If only two players are left, the game ends anyway.
  4. The Unmasking. One by one, the roles of the last players are shown. If only Loyal are left, the Loyal win and share the Purse. If a Forsworn is still in the game, the Forsworn win and take what the Purse fell short.
  5. The last betrayal. If two or more Forsworn are left, each one secretly chooses HONOUR or BETRAY. If they all choose HONOUR, they share the winnings. If one chooses BETRAY, they take everything. If two or more choose BETRAY, nobody gets anything.
  6. The awards. Everyone’s role is shown. Then come up to six awards for the night, such as The Grand Deceiver, The Sharpest Eye, Barking Up the Wrong Tree and The Ghost Who Saw.

Trials

The game picks the Trials in a random order, so no two evenings are the same.

Most Likely To
Every phone shows a prompt. Vote for the person it fits best, then see who everyone chose. There’s no prize, only a reputation.
Rapid Quiz
Fifteen questions: five easy, five medium and five brutal. Type your answer before the clock runs out. You get a point for every right answer, and one more if you’re the fastest.
Longest Word
You get twelve letters. Type the longest real word you can make from them.
Closest Guess
Each question has a number for its answer. Type your guess, and the closest guesses score.
Memory Wall
Twelve words appear, then disappear. Type every one you can remember.
Snap
A symbol changes every few seconds. When it matches the one before, tap SNAP. A wrong tap counts against you.
Rock Paper Scissors Royale
Players are paired up at random, and each pair plays rock, paper, scissors on their phones. Lose and you’re out. It goes on until one player is left.
One Leg
Everyone stands on one leg. If your other foot touches the floor, tap the button on your phone to say you’re out. The last one standing wins.
Screen Charades
One player at a time gets a secret word on their phone and acts it out. When someone guesses it, the actor taps their name.
Hum It
Like charades, but your phone gives you a song to hum.
Draw It
Like charades, but you draw the word on paper, on a board or in the air. You can’t use letters or numbers.
The Statue
The players still in the game freeze like statues. The Ghosts can do anything except touch them to make them move or laugh.
Two Truths and a Lie
One player says three things about themselves. Two are true and one is a lie. Everyone else votes on their phone for the lie.
Higher or Lower
You see a question and a number. Is the real answer higher or lower than that number?
What Changed?
Twelve symbols appear, then disappear. When they come back, something is different. Tap what changed. The first right answer scores double.

The quiz, charades, songs, pictures and guessing games draw on a bank of thousands of questions and prompts, and nothing repeats within a game.

Classic: the longer, more immersive evening

Every Table can also be played Classic, and you choose which way to play when you open it. It’s the same game, run differently, and it takes about 3 to 3½ hours for 8 to 12 players. Here is what changes:

  • A Keeper runs it. The Keeper is the person who runs the game from one phone and reads its lines aloud, like a presenter. They know who the Forsworn are, so they don’t play. The host can still play. They plan the game, then hand the phone to a friend, who becomes the Keeper.
  • The Oath is sworn blindfolded. The Keeper walks behind the chairs, and a tap on the shoulder makes someone Forsworn. Then the Forsworn take off their blindfolds to see each other.
  • Votes are chalked on small boards. The boards are turned round one at a time, and the TV keeps count. In the Trials, players shout or chalk their answers.
  • Nights are blindfolded. The Keeper wakes the Forsworn, who point to their victim in silence. Then the Keeper taps the victim, who reads the screen and says nothing until dawn.
  • The Keeper chooses more. They choose how many rounds to play, which Trials, whether there are Ghosts, and whether to have an Interval, a 10-minute break before the vote. There are extra twists too, such as a whispered offer to change sides and secret tasks for the Forsworn. If the Forsworn betray each other at the end, the winnings can pass to the Ghosts who named a Forsworn.
  • Dinner fits in. If you switch on dinner in courses, the game makes time for a starter early on, the main course mid-game, and dessert just before the final.
  • You need a TV for Classic, because it’s where the players see the game.

What you need

  • 8 to 24 players. 8 to 16 is the sweet spot.
  • Between 45 minutes and two hours. It takes about 45 minutes for 8 to 10 players, an hour to an hour and a quarter for 12 to 16, and an hour and a half to two hours for 20 to 24. Classic takes longer, about 3 to 3½ hours for 8 to 12. When the Keeper sets up a Classic game, their phone gives an estimate for your group.
  • A prize, such as a bag of chocolate coins.
  • A phone each. A TV is optional, but it makes the game a show. Connect a laptop to it, or cast to it.
  • For Classic, you need a TV with a laptop connected or casting to it, and a phone for the Keeper. Every guest needs a blindfold, and sleep masks work well. Every guest also needs a small chalkboard and chalk, or a whiteboard and pen.

In short, The Betrayal is a hidden-role party game for 8 to 24 players in one room. It’s best with 8 to 16, and it’s played on everyone’s own phone with no app to install. A game takes about 45 minutes for 8 to 10 players, and up to about two hours for 24. A Table costs £10 for one party, or £20 a month as a Regular.
