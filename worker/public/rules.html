How to play

Everything that happens at a night of The Betrayal, from the first oath to the last betrayal. You don’t need to learn any of it before the night: every phone tells its player what to do, when. But if you like to know what you’re buying, here is the whole game.

This page describes the game as most people play it: everyone on their own phone. For a longer, more immersive evening, there’s Classic, with a Keeper, blindfolds and chalk.

The idea

A few of your guests are secretly Forsworn. Everyone else is one of the Loyal. Every night the Forsworn poison someone, and every day the table votes to throw someone out. The Loyal win if they find every Forsworn before the end. The Forsworn win if even one of them is still at the table when the game finishes.

In between, you play party games for a shared Purse and for protection, you argue, you make alliances, and you lie to people you’ve known for years. A typical game lasts an hour or two. Play it with drinks and snacks.

Joining

The host opens the Table with their booking code and types their name. Everyone else scans the QR code on the TV, or goes to thebetrayal.co.uk/join and types the six-character Table code and their name. There’s nothing to install and no account to make. The TV is optional: without one, the host just reads the code out. When everyone’s in, the host taps Start.

Who’s who

The Loyal are most of the table. They don’t know who anyone else is. Their job is to work out who the Forsworn are and vote them out, with nothing to go on but conversation, voting patterns and gut feeling.

The Forsworn are about one guest in four or five, and their phones tell them who the others are. Each night they choose who is poisoned. By day they act like the Loyal, steer the vote, and quietly sabotage the Trials.

The host has a secret role like everyone else and never sees anyone else’s. Their phone also moves the evening on, beginning the next part or calling a break. The game plans its own Trials and Reckonings. (If a game can’t carry on, say a phone dies and nobody remembers who was Forsworn, the host’s tools can show the roles as a last resort; the phone asks three times first, and everyone is told at the end.)

The night begins with the Oath. Every phone asks its player, privately: would you like to be Forsworn? Yes, make me Forsworn; I’d rather be Loyal; or Either way. The roles are dealt from the answers (volunteers first), though nobody is promised what they asked for. Then each player looks at their role with their phone held close.

Nobody is told how many Forsworn there are. Players only find out as Forsworn are exiled and revealed.

A round

The game is played in rounds, planned from the size of the table so that the final starts with five or fewer. Each round goes like this:

  1. A Trial. A party game played on the phones: a quiz, a guessing game, charades and so on (all of them are listed below). Each question closes by itself when everyone has answered or the clock runs out. Trials add to the Purse, and most give Sanctuary to the winner. The evening opens with Most Likely To, as the icebreaker.
  2. The Reckoning. A few minutes of open debate on a timer, then everyone votes on their phone, in secret. The vote closes when everyone’s in. The player with the most votes is exiled. A tie goes to a revote between the tied players; a second tie is settled by lot. At a table bigger than sixteen, there are two exiles.
  3. The reveal. The exiled player’s role is shown: Forsworn or Loyal. They can type one last line for the TV on the way out.
  4. The night. Heads down. The Forsworn choose a victim on their phones (two while twelve or more are left at the table, three while twenty or more are). Everyone else taps someone too, so nobody at the table can tell who is choosing. The Forsworn can also whisper to each other in set phrases (“Vote with me tomorrow”). A player holding Sanctuary can’t be poisoned.
  5. Dawn. The victim learns first, in secret on their phone. Then the host reveals the night to everyone. The victim was poisoned: they stand, announce it and get thirty seconds of last words, then leave the table. If the table voted them most likely to something, the TV remembers that too.

The host can call a short break whenever they like, or pause the game, which stops every clock, for the doorbell or a top-up. If someone is taken ill, the host can take them out of the game without anyone learning their role.

The dead

Nobody goes home when they’re out. Exiled and poisoned players become Ghosts: they stay at the party and watch the game on their phones. They can’t vote, but at every Reckoning they can call who they think goes, and the table is told which Ghosts called it. In The Statue, the Ghosts do their worst to make the living laugh.

Sanctuary

Sanctuary protects its holder from that night’s poison. It’s the prize in most Trials, and it lasts one night. At most a quarter of the table can hold one at the same time. The host chooses at the start whether Sanctuary is won in public, named for everyone, or in secret, so only the winner knows they’re safe. A Forsworn who wins a secret Sanctuary gets nothing, and is the only one who knows.

Twists

Each comes once in the evening, and the phones explain it when it happens. The host can switch them all off before the start, for a plainer first game.

  • The Casting Vote, about halfway: its Trial’s winner gets it instead of Sanctuary, and nobody else knows who. If a Reckoning ties and they voted for one of the tied players, their vote decides it.
  • The Temptation: everyone chooses, in secret, PURSE or SELF. Exactly one SELF takes Sanctuary and the Purse grows. Nobody, and it grows double. Two or more, and nobody gets it, and the Purse shrinks.
  • The Crown, on the last round before the final: named for everyone, it brings Sanctuary that night, and once, at a Reckoning, its holder can lay it down to be safe from that exile.
  • The Confessor, in the final: the winner of its Trial is told, privately, the name of a Forsworn still at the table.

The Purse

Every Trial adds to a shared Purse, up to its target: the better the table plays, the more goes in. The winners take it at the end: the Loyal take what they built, but the Forsworn take what it fell short of the target, so every point they quietly sabotage is theirs. Each wrong answer from a Forsworn costs the Purse, and the table hears how many there were, never whose. A Loyal player can guess freely, so a shrinking Purse is a clue.

There is also a prize, chosen by the host: something worth lying to your friends for. If they don’t choose one, it’s chocolate coins, one for every 100 points the winners take from the Purse. The host can set better prizes for a fuller Purse, or, if the Forsworn win, an emptier one; the table knows the Purse decides the prize, but not what it is.

The end game

The last round is the final, with five or fewer at the table.

  1. A last Trial, for the Purse and perhaps something more. There’s no Sanctuary in the final, because there is no night left to survive.
  2. The Reckoning, without reveals. The table votes a player out, but this time their role is not shown.
  3. Trust or Doubt. With four or fewer left, each survivor chooses on their phone, in secret: TRUST if they believe every Forsworn is gone, DOUBT if not. Only a unanimous TRUST ends the game. Otherwise, it’s back to the Reckoning for another exile, and another vote.
  4. The Unmasking. One by one, the last players’ roles are turned over. If only Loyal are left, the Loyal win and share the Purse. If a Forsworn is still at the table, the Forsworn win, and take what the Purse fell short.
  5. The last betrayal. If two or more Forsworn survive, each chooses in secret: HONOUR or BETRAY. If they all honour the pact, they share the winnings. If one betrays, they take everything. If two or more betray, nobody does.
  6. The awards. Everyone’s role, then up to six awards for the night: The Grand Deceiver, The Sharpest Eye, Barking Up the Wrong Tree, The Ghost Who Saw and more.

Trials

The game deals them in a random order, so no two evenings run the same.

Most Likely To
A prompt on every phone. Vote for the person it fits best, and see the verdict. There’s no prize, only a reputation.
Rapid Quiz
Eight questions, easy to brutal. Type your answer before the clock runs out: a point for every right answer, and one more for the fastest.
Longest Word
Twelve letters. Type the longest real word you can make from them.
Closest Guess
A question with a number for an answer. The closest guesses score.
Memory Wall
Twelve words appear, then vanish. Type every one you remember.
Snap
A symbol changes every few seconds. When it matches the one before, tap SNAP. A wrong tap costs you.
Rock Paper Scissors Royale
Paired off at random, choose on your phone. Lose and you’re out, until one is left standing.
One Leg
Everyone up, on one leg. Put a foot down and tap “I’m out”. Last one standing wins.
Screen Charades
One player at a time gets a secret word on their phone and acts it out. When someone guesses, the actor taps who.
Hum It
Like charades, but the phone gives you a song to hum.
Pictionary
Like charades, but you draw it: on paper, a board, or in the air. No letters, no numbers.
The Statue
The living freeze. The Ghosts may do anything, short of touching, to make them move or laugh.
Two Truths and a Lie
One player tells three things about themselves. Everyone else votes on their phone for the lie.
Higher or Lower
A question and a number: is the real answer higher or lower?
What Changed?
Twelve symbols appear. They vanish, and come back with something different. Tap what changed; the first right answer scores double.

The quiz, charades, songs, pictures and guessing games draw on a bank of thousands of questions and prompts, and nothing repeats within a game.

Classic: the longer, more immersive evening

Every Table can also be played Classic; you choose when you open it. It’s the same game, run differently, over about 3 to 3½ hours for 8 to 12 players:

  • A Keeper runs it from one phone, like a presenter, and doesn’t play: they know who the Forsworn are, and their phone gives them every line to read. (The host can still play: plan the game, then hand the phone to a friend to keep.)
  • The Oath is sworn blindfolded: the Keeper walks behind the chairs, and a tap on the shoulder makes someone Forsworn. The Forsworn take off their blindfolds to see each other.
  • Votes are chalked on small boards and turned one at a time, while the TV keeps count. Answers in the games are shouted or chalked.
  • Nights are blindfolded: the Keeper wakes the Forsworn, who point to their victim in silence; the Keeper taps the victim, who reads the screen and says nothing until dawn.
  • The Keeper chooses more: how many rounds, which Trials, Ghosts on or off, an Interval, and extra twists such as a whispered offer to change sides and secret tasks for the Forsworn. If the Forsworn betray each other at the end, the winnings can pass to the Ghosts who named a Forsworn.
  • Dinner fits in: switch on dinner in courses, and the game serves a starter early on, the main course mid-game, and dessert just before the final.
  • The TV is essential in Classic: it’s where the players see the game.

What you need

  • 8 to 24 players. 8 to 16 is the sweet spot.
  • An hour or two: about an hour for 8 to 10 players, an hour and a half for 12 to 16, and about two hours for 20 to 24. Classic takes longer, about 3 to 3½ hours for 8 to 12, and the Keeper’s setup gives an estimate for your group.
  • A prize.
  • A phone each. A TV, with a laptop connected or casting to it, makes it a show, but it’s optional.
  • For Classic: a TV with a laptop connected or casting to it, a phone for the Keeper, a blindfold for every guest (sleep masks work well), and a small chalkboard and chalk, or a whiteboard and pen, for every guest.

In short: The Betrayal is a hidden-role party game for 8 to 24 players in one room, best with 8 to 16, played on everyone’s own phone with no app to install.
