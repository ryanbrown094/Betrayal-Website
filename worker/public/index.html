How an evening goes

  1. You open your TableEnter your booking code and your name on your phone. This opens your Table, the game everyone joins, and makes you the host. You still play. Then choose a prize, or keep the usual one, chocolate coins.
  2. Everyone scans inGuests scan the QR code on the TV, or go to thebetrayal.co.uk/join and type the six-character Table code. Nobody needs an app or an account, just a name.
  3. The OathEach phone asks its player, in secret, whether they would like to be Forsworn. Then everyone sees their own role. The Forsworn learn who the other Forsworn are. Everyone else is Loyal and doesn’t know who anyone is.
  4. The TrialsTrials are party games on the phones, like quizzes and guessing games. The game times them and keeps the score. Each one adds to the Purse, the prize pot everyone shares. The Forsworn want it to stay low, so they quietly play badly.
  5. The ReckoningEveryone argues about who might be Forsworn, then votes in secret. The player with the most votes is exiled, which means voted out. Everyone sees whether they were Forsworn or Loyal.
  6. NightEveryone looks at their phone and taps a name, so nobody can tell who is really choosing. Only the Forsworn’s choice counts. At dawn, the player they chose learns they have been poisoned and is out of the game.
  7. The masks come offThe final begins when five players are left, or four in a game of eight. The voting goes on until everyone still in agrees to trust each other. Then every role is shown, and the winners take the prize.

As the host, your phone just tells you when to start the next part. The game does everything else.

Every phone shows its player a secret role and a vote. A TV is optional. If you use one, it shows the questions and the big reveals, with music.

Before you ask

What is The Betrayal?

The Betrayal is a party game for 8 to 24 players in one room, and it’s best with 8 to 16. Everyone plays on their own phone, in the browser. A few players are secretly the Forsworn, the traitors. Everyone else is Loyal, and they have one evening to find the Forsworn. You play party games called Trials on the phones, such as quizzes, and they fill a shared prize pot called the Purse. Each day everyone votes in secret to exile, or vote out, one player. Each night the Forsworn poison someone, which puts that player out of the game. A game takes about 45 minutes for 8 to 10 players, and up to about two hours for 24. It costs £10 for one party, or £20 a month as a Regular.

Does everyone need a phone?

Yes. Everyone plays on their own phone, in the browser, with nothing to install. If you would rather put the phones away, play Classic instead. It’s a longer, more immersive evening of about three hours. One person, the Keeper, runs the game from one phone. Everyone else wears a blindfold at night and chalks their vote on a small board. You choose which way to play when you open your Table.

What does booking a Table get me?

A Table is one game for one party, and it costs £10. It stays open for 48 hours from when the game starts. So you can open it early, invite everyone and set up at your own pace, then play into the small hours.

What’s a Regular?

A Regular pays £20 a month for one booking code that starts a new game whenever you like. You can have one game open at a time, and each one stays open for 48 hours from when it starts. The subscription renews every month until you cancel. You can cancel at any time with “Manage subscription”.

Does the host get to play?

Yes. The host gets a secret role like everyone else. Their phone also says when to start the next part and when to take a break, but it never shows anyone’s role. Classic is different. There, the Keeper runs the game like a presenter and knows who the Forsworn are from the start, so the Keeper doesn’t play. If you’re the host and want to play Classic, ask a friend to be the Keeper. You plan the game on your phone, then hand it to your friend before anyone sits down. Your friend presses Start, so you never see the roles.

How long does it take?

About 45 minutes for 8 to 10 players, an hour to an hour and a quarter for 12 to 16, and an hour and a half to two hours for 20 to 24. Classic, the longer way to play, takes about 3 to 3½ hours for 8 to 12 players. If you’re eating, Classic can fit dinner in courses between its rounds. When the Keeper sets up a Classic game, their phone shows an estimate for your group.

Why “The Betrayal”?

The game starts with an oath. Every guest swears to be loyal to everyone else at the table, but a few of them don’t mean it. They are the Forsworn. Each night they poison someone, and the Loyal have until the final round to find out who betrayed them.

Is it like The Traitors?

The idea is similar. A few players are secretly traitors, who are called the Forsworn here, and everyone else tries to find them. Each day there’s a vote to throw someone out, and each night there’s a poisoning. The difference is that you play it yourselves, at home or at a party, on your own phones, and everyone in the room is in the game. The Betrayal is an original game. It isn’t connected to The Traitors, the BBC or the programme’s makers. If you’d like to run something like it, here’s how to host a traitors-style game night at home.

Can we play it at a work Christmas party?

Yes. It suits an office party, a Christmas do, a birthday or a hen party, as well as a night in with friends. You need 8 to 24 people and a phone each, plus a big screen if the room has one. Nobody installs anything or makes an account. For bigger groups, read our guide to party games for large groups.

How do the Forsworn kill?

With poison. The player they choose finds out in secret, on their phone, during the night. At dawn they stand up and tell everyone. They get thirty seconds of last words, then leave the table. In Classic, the Keeper quietly taps them while everyone is blindfolded.

What if the Wi-Fi drops?

Every phone reconnects by itself and carries on where it left off. That’s because the game runs on our server, not on anyone’s phone. If someone’s battery dies, they can join again on another phone, and the host lets them back in.

Who wins the Purse?

The Purse is the prize pot that the Trials fill, up to a target. If the Loyal win, they take what is in the Purse. If the Forsworn win, they take what it fell short of its target. So every point the Forsworn quietly lose in the Trials is a point for them. More in the rules.
