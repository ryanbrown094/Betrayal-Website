Before you ask

What is The Betrayal?

The Betrayal is a party game for 8 to 24 players in one room, best with 8 to 16, played on everyone’s own phone in the browser. A few players are secretly the Forsworn; the rest, the Loyal, have one evening to find them. Trials (quizzes and party games on the phones) fill a shared Purse, a secret vote exiles someone each day, and each night the Forsworn poison someone. A game takes about an hour for 8 to 10 players, and up to about two hours for 24. It costs £10 for one party, or £20 a month as a Regular.

Does everyone need a phone?

Yes: everyone plays on their own, in the browser, with nothing to install. For a longer, more immersive evening with phones away, play Classic: a Keeper runs it from one phone, guests wear blindfolds at night and chalk their votes, and it takes about three hours. You choose when you open your Table.

What does booking a Table get me?

One Table for one party (£10). It stays open for 48 hours from when you first open it, so you can set up in the afternoon and play into the small hours.

What’s a Regular?

A Regular pays £20 a month and gets one booking code that starts a new game whenever you like, one game at a time, each open for 48 hours. It renews monthly until you cancel, which you can do any time with “Manage subscription”.

Does the host get to play?

Yes. The host’s phone also says when to begin the next part and when to take a break, but it never shows anyone’s role. In Classic, the game’s Keeper runs it like a presenter, and knows who the Forsworn are from the start, so the Keeper doesn’t play. The host can still play: ask a friend to keep. You plan the game on your phone, then hand it over before anyone sits down, and your friend presses Start, so you never see the roles.

How long does it take?

About an hour for 8 to 10 players, an hour and a half for 12 to 16, and about two hours for 20 to 24. Classic, the longer way to play, takes about 3 to 3½ hours for 8 to 12 players, with dinner in courses between its rounds if you’re eating; the Keeper’s setup shows an estimate for your table.

Why “The Betrayal”?

It starts with an oath. Every guest swears to be loyal to the table, and a few of them don’t mean it: they are the Forsworn. Each night they poison someone, and the Loyal have until the final to find out who betrayed them.

Is it like The Traitors?

The idea is similar: a few players are secretly traitors (here they are the Forsworn), everyone else tries to find them, there’s a vote to throw someone out each day and a poisoning each night. The difference is that you play it yourselves, at home or at a party, on your own phones, and everyone in the room is in the game. The Betrayal is an original game. It isn’t connected to The Traitors, the BBC or the programme’s makers. If you’d like to run something like it, here’s how to host a traitors-style game night at home.

Can we play it at a work Christmas party?

Yes. It suits an office party, a Christmas do, a birthday or a hen party as well as a night in with friends: 8 to 24 people, a phone each, and a big screen if the room has one. Nobody installs anything or makes an account. For bigger groups, read our guide to party games for large groups.

How do the Forsworn kill?

With poison. The victim finds out in secret on their phone during the night. At dawn they stand up, announce it, get thirty seconds of last words, then leave the table. (In Classic, the Keeper taps them while everyone is blindfolded.)

What if the Wi-Fi drops?

Every phone reconnects by itself and picks up where it was: the game lives on our server, not on anyone’s phone. If someone’s battery dies, they rejoin on another phone and the host lets them back in.

Who wins the Purse?

The Loyal, if they win, take the Purse they built. The Forsworn, if they win, take what it fell short of its target, so every point they quietly sabotage is theirs. More in the rules.
