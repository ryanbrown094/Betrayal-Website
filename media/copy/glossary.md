# The Betrayal: words, names and naming

The words the game, the site and every piece of marketing use, how to write them, and the words never to use.

## The name

**The Betrayal.** Always with “The”, and both words capitalised: “The Betrayal”, never “Betrayal” alone. Where a space can’t go (URLs, file names, handles), write `thebetrayal`. The site is **thebetrayal.co.uk**. Never pair the name with another game’s or show’s name.

## The tagline

**The party game of loyalty and lies.** This is the one descriptor line, everywhere: the press kit, the ads, the site and the brand guidelines. Where only a couple of words fit (an eyebrow above the name), write **a party game**. The Betrayal is a party game, not a dinner-party game: it suits a dinner, drinks and snacks, a birthday, a games night or an office do. Dinner is an option, never the point, so don’t call it “a dinner-party game” and don’t lead with food.

## Dinner is optional

Play it over dinner if you like: the game can serve dinner in courses between the rounds (a starter, the main course and dessert). It’s a switch the host turns on, and it’s **off by default**. In Modern it’s **Dinner in courses** in the lobby; in Classic it’s **Dinner** under Customise (three courses, one dinner break, or none, and none is pre-set). With it off, the host can still call a short break. Mention it as a choice (“if you’re eating together”), not as the shape of the evening.

## The idea in one line

Everyone at the table swears an oath. A few of them are lying. They are the **Forsworn**, and the **Loyal** have one evening to find them.

## Two ways to play: Modern first

Every Table can be played two ways, chosen when it’s opened. **Modern is the headline**: lead with it everywhere. Classic is the other way to play, for groups who’d rather put their phones away. It gets a line or a clearly marked section, never the lead.

- **Modern:** everyone plays on their own phone. Guests scan the QR code on the TV, or go to **thebetrayal.co.uk/join** and type the **Table code** and their name, like a pub-quiz app. The Oath is a question on every phone; roles, votes, the night and poisonings arrive in secret on each phone; every Trial is played there. The **host** plays like everyone else; their phone also moves the evening on. There’s no Keeper and nothing to buy, and the TV is optional.
- **Classic:** a TV with a laptop, and one phone runs the game. **The Keeper** reads the script and doesn’t play (a friend can keep so the host can play); guests wear blindfolds at night and chalk their votes on small boards; the TV puts on the show.

## Two tiers of words

### Public words

Public copy (ads, the press kit, video scripts, outreach, the content calendar, store listings, the public parts of the marketing pack) uses only these:

**the Forsworn · the Loyal · the Oath · Trial · the Reckoning · Sanctuary · the Purse · Ghost · the Unmasking · the Keeper** (Classic only)

Plus plain words (the host, exile, night, dawn, dinner, the Interval, oath, candle, phone; and, for Classic, blindfold and chalkboard) and the product words (a Table, Book a Table, booking code, a Regular, Become a Regular, Table code, /join, QR code, Modern, Classic, the Short game).

### In-game words

**the Casting Vote · the Crown · the Temptation · the Whisper · the Last Offer · Errands · the Confessor · Trust or Doubt · Honour or Betray**

These belong to the game. Each phone (and the TV, if there is one) explains each one when it happens, and the internal game guide explains them all. In public copy, never name them: call them **twists the game explains when they happen**. The Confessor is never named or described in public, not even as a hint.

## Capitals

Capitalised: **The Betrayal**, **the Forsworn**, **the Loyal**, **Ghost**, **the Keeper**, **the Oath** (the opening scene), **Trial**, **the Reckoning**, **the Interval**, **Sanctuary**, **the Purse**, **the Unmasking**; in-game, **the Casting Vote**, **the Crown**, **the Temptation**, **the Whisper**, **the Last Offer**, **Errand**, **the Confessor** (internal), **Trust or Doubt**, **Honour or Betray**; product, **Table**, **Table code**, **Regular**, **Modern**, **Classic**, **the Short game**, **Customise**.

Lower case: the host, exile, night, dawn, poison, oath (the promise itself: “everyone swore an oath”), seal, candle, phone, blindfold, chalkboard, dinner, starter, main course, dessert, booking code, QR code.

What people choose is written in capitals: PURSE, SELF, TRUST, DOUBT.

## Length: say it honestly

- **Best at 8 to 16 players.** The game takes 8 to 24.
- **8 to 12 players:** about 3 to 3½ hours. Stopping for dinner adds half an hour (one break) to an hour (three courses).
- **The Short game** (Classic): about 2 to 2½ hours for 8 to 10 players (one Trial a round, no Interval, no dinner break).
- **Bigger tables run longer:** about 4¼ hours for 16, and 5 to 6 for 20 and up.
- State the capacity as a fact; never sell it as a benefit. No “up to 24 players, one very long night”.

## Marks and type

The brand guidelines have the detail; in brief:

- **The primary lockup** (the hooded figure and the wordmark) signs everything where the name needs to be said.
- **The secondary mark** is the cracked wax seal, *the broken oath*: `the-betrayal-seal.svg` / `.png` in full colour (wine wax, a candle embossed, a rose crack) and `the-betrayal-seal-mono-rose.svg`, `-ivory.svg` and `-ink.svg` in one colour. Use it for the favicon and app icon, social avatars, stickers and packaging seals. It never replaces the lockup.
- **Type:** Grenze Gotisch (blackletter) only for the wordmark and the big TV moments. Headings in IM Fell DW Pica. Body and UI in Alegreya Sans; eyebrows in Alegreya Sans SC.

## Voice

Candlelit, dry, a little theatrical, never gory. Short sentences. The game speaks to the table like someone who knows more than they’re saying: “Everyone here swore an oath tonight. Not everyone meant it.” Use British spelling and British words (Interval, not intermission; queue, not line). The calls to action are **Book a Table** and **Become a Regular**. Sell Modern first: everyone plays on their own phone, and there’s nothing to buy or learn.

## The Oath

The Oath is the opening scene and the brand’s most theatrical moment. Keep it theatrical, never religious: no crosses, altars, priests or holy books. In Modern, show it as a secret question on a phone held close in candlelight: *Would you like to be Forsworn?* In Classic, show it with hands flat on the table, blindfolds and candlelight. Capital O for the scene (“the Oath”); lower case for the promise itself (“everyone swore an oath”).

## Never

- Use the name, logo, music, catchphrases, presenter, set or format words of any TV programme. That includes “traitor”, “faithful”, “round table”, “banishment”, “shield”, “dagger”, “seer”, “recruit”, “ultimatum” and “mission” as game terms.
- Use Hasbro’s Betrayal vocabulary (“haunt”, “omen”, “heroes”, “the house on the hill”), and never write “Betrayal at…”.
- Name an in-game word in public material, or reveal the Confessor anywhere public.
- Use the words in the “never say” list below.
- Call the Modern host a Keeper, or lead any public piece with Classic.
- Call Classic old, old-fashioned or the original version. It’s a choice of style, for groups who’d rather put their phones away.
- Sell the table size or the length of the night as a benefit.
- Show a face under the hood.
- Call it “a dinner-party game”, or sell dinner as the point of the evening. Dinner is an option.
