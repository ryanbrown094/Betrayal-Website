# The Betrayal: the complete game (internal)

> **Internal: this document contains every secret in the game.** It’s for you and the people making marketing with you. Anything customer-facing uses only the public words (the Forsworn, the Loyal, the Oath, Trial, the Reckoning, Sanctuary, the Purse, Ghost, the Unmasking, and the Keeper for Classic only, plus plain words like the host, exile, night, dawn, dinner and the Interval). Everything else here (the Casting Vote, the Crown, the Temptation, the Whisper, the Last Offer, Errands, the Confessor, Trust or Doubt, Honour or Betray) is in-game vocabulary: in public it’s one of the “twists the game explains when they happen”, and the Confessor is never named or hinted at. The two lists are in `glossary-and-naming.md`.

This guide follows the decisions of 29 September 2026 (the Table and the Regular, the Oath, the Casting Vote as a tie-break, PURSE or SELF, dinner in courses, and **Modern as the headline way to play**). Sections 1 to 9 describe **Modern**, where everyone plays on their own phone. **Classic**, the old-fashioned way with one phone, a Keeper, blindfolds and chalkboards, has its own section (10). The numbers per table size are in `table-sizes.csv`, the Trials in `trials-modern.csv` and `trials.csv` (Classic), the question banks in `question-banks.md`, and every setting for both ways to play in `setup-guide.md`.

## 1. The idea in one paragraph

A dinner-party game for 8 to 24 guests (best with 8 to 16), and in Modern the host plays too. A few guests are secretly **Forsworn**, the killers; everyone else is **Loyal**. Each round the table plays a party game (a **Trial**) for a shared **Purse** and for protection, then votes someone out at **the Reckoning**. Each night the Forsworn kill. The Loyal win if every Forsworn is gone at the end; the Forsworn win if even one is still at the table. In **Modern**, everyone plays on their own phone: guests scan the QR code on the TV, or go to **thebetrayal.co.uk/join** and type the Table code and their name, like a pub-quiz app. Roles, votes, Trials, the night and poisonings all arrive in secret on each phone. The server runs the game and plans its own Trials, courses and Reckonings; the host’s phone only moves the evening on. No blindfolds, no chalkboards, no Keeper, and the TV is optional.

## 2. The shape of an evening (Modern)

1. **Open the Table (the host, before or as guests arrive).** Enter the booking code, choose **Modern**, type your name, **Open the Table**. Share the Table code, or put the TV on so guests can scan its QR code. While they join, the host makes five quick choices (murder or poison, secret or public Sanctuary, the prize, twists on or off, dinner in courses) and presses **Start**. Details in `setup-guide.md`.
2. **The Oath.** Every phone asks the same question in secret: **“Would you like to be Forsworn?”** (Yes, make me Forsworn / I’d rather be Loyal / Either way). Nobody sees anyone’s answer. The game uses the answers when it deals the roles, but can’t always give everyone what they asked for.
3. **Roles.** Each guest holds their phone close and taps to see their role. The Forsworn see each other’s names on their phones (a lone Forsworn is told they’re alone). Everyone taps “Got it” and the role hides again.
4. **Rounds**, each: Trial(s) → the result and prizes → (a break) → the Reckoning → reveal → night → dawn. Dinner can come in three courses between the rounds (4.3).
5. **The final**, with five or fewer: exiles are no longer revealed, and the table votes TRUST or DOUBT on their phones.
6. **The Unmasking**: every seal turned, the winners, the prize the Purse has earned, then **Honour or Betray** if two or more Forsworn survived.
7. **Awards**, up to six. Then the host can **play again with the same people**.

**How long:** 8 to 12 players, about 3 to 4 hours with dinner. Bigger tables run longer, because they need more rounds to get down to five: 20 or more, about 6 hours. `table-sizes.csv` lists every size. (Classic also has a Short game of about 2 hours for 8 to 10.) In marketing, state the length honestly and never sell a big table or a long night as a benefit.

### The host plays too

The person who opens the Table is **the host**, and plays like everyone else, with a secret role. Their phone also carries a small host card: **Begin** the next Trial (or call **a short break first**, 10 minutes, with **+5 minutes** and **End the break**), move on to the Reckoning, **Reveal the night**, **On to the next round**. It never shows anyone’s role. The game plans its own Trials, courses and Reckonings; the host only decides when the room is ready. **Tools** on the host’s phone handle the awkward moments: someone has left the party, a phone that lost its place asks to be let back in, hand the host’s controls to someone else, play the music on this phone if there’s no TV, and skip or settle a step if someone is stuck.

### The TV (optional)

Any laptop cast to the TV, at `thebetrayal.co.uk/t/CODE`. It shows the QR code and Table code for joining, then the questions, the votes, the reveals and a running Purse, with music, and an optional narrator that reads each new screen aloud. It never knows a secret. Without a TV, the phones carry everything, and the host’s phone can play the music.

## 3. Who’s who

| role | who | knows | wins when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Loyal** | most guests | only their own role | every Forsworn is gone at the end |
| **Forsworn** | about one in four or five, dealt by the game with the Oath answers in mind (2 for 8–10 players, 3 for 11–15, 4 for 16–22, 5 for 23–24) | each other | at least one Forsworn is still at the table at the end |
| **Ghost** | anyone exiled or killed | what they see; they no longer vote or play Trials | they win or lose with their side; they still call it at each Reckoning and haunt the living in the Statue, and the Ghost who calls the most exiles can win an award |
| **the host** | whoever opened the Table | only their own role | plays like everyone else; their phone also moves the evening on |

Nobody is told how many Forsworn there are.

## 4. A round, step by step

### 4.1 The Trial
- The game picks the next Trial itself; the host’s phone shows it (“Begin: Rapid Quiz”) and the host begins it when the room is ready.
- Every phone shows the rules, then the Trial: typed quiz answers, number guesses, higher or lower, votes, words from letters, a memory list, rock-paper-scissors bouts, Snap. In the acting Trials (Screen Charades, Hum It, Pictionary) the performer gets the word or song **privately on their phone**, and taps who guessed it. In Two Truths and a Lie, the teller chooses which of the three is the lie on their phone and everyone votes for it on theirs. Clocks run on every screen, and the game moves on when everyone has answered or time is up.
- **Points** go into the Purse. The Loyal, if they win, take the Purse they built; the Forsworn, if they win, take what it **fell short** of its target, so every point they sabotage is theirs. The prize tiers follow the same amount.
- **Wrong answers** by a Forsworn cost the Purse 50 each (every Forsworn’s phone reminds them). The room sees the total the Forsworn gave away, never whose.
- The winner takes the round’s prize (Sanctuary, or on the right round the Casting Vote or the Crown). Most Likely To carries no prize: just a reputation.

### 4.2 Prizes: Sanctuary (every round)
- **Sanctuary** protects its holder from that night’s kill. It lasts one night. No Sanctuaries in the final (no night left).
- Set by the host before the game: **public** (everyone sees who won it) or **secret** (only the winner knows, on their phone).
- If a Forsworn wins Sanctuary it does nothing (Forsworn can’t be killed). With secret Sanctuary, only they learn so.

### 4.3 Breaks and dinner
- **A short break** (the Interval, 10 minutes): the host can call one before any Trial or Reckoning. Private chats, deals, accusations. The host can add 5 minutes or end it early.
- **Dinner, served in three courses** (on or off at setup): a starter, a main and dessert, each served between the rounds. With courses off, the host can still call a short break.

### 4.4 The Reckoning
- **Talk. Accuse. Defend.** A few minutes of open debate, with a clock on every phone and the TV.
- Then everyone votes **on their phone**, for anyone still alive. The counts are shown when the vote closes.
- Most votes is **exiled**. A tie goes to a revote between the tied players, unless the Casting Vote breaks it (section 5); a tie that won’t break is settled by lot.
- **Ghosts’ call:** the Ghosts vote too, on who they think will go. It doesn’t count, but it’s remembered, and the best Ghost can win an award.
- **More than sixteen at the table:** two exiles.
- **Reveal:** the exiled player’s role is shown, Forsworn or Loyal.

### 4.5 The night
- **Heads down.** Every living player taps a name on their phone, so nobody can tell who is really choosing. Only the Forsworn’s choices count.
- **Victims per night** grow with the table so the final still starts with five: 1 below 12 alive, up to 2 from 12, up to 3 from 20.
- **The victim learns in secret, on their phone.** **Poison** (default): “Say nothing yet”; when the host reveals the night, they get their **last words**. **Murder**: they say nothing and leave the table quietly at dawn.
- Sanctuary saves its holder: “nobody died”, and the table doesn’t learn why.
- Five left after the Reckoning: **no night**. The final round starts.

### 4.6 Dawn
The host taps **Reveal the night**: every phone (and the TV) shows who died, one more candle goes out, and the poisoned have their last words.

## 5. Powers and twists: Sanctuary every round, everything else once a game

| power | when | what it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Sanctuary** | every round, from Trials | safe from that night’s kill |
| **Temptation** | once a game, twists on or off | offered after a Trial: everyone chooses **PURSE or SELF** in secret on their phone. Exactly one SELF: they take Sanctuary and the Purse grows. Nobody: the Purse grows by double. Two or more: nobody gets Sanctuary and the Purse shrinks |
| **Casting Vote** | once a game, twists on | won in a Trial; only the winner knows. If a Reckoning ties and they voted for one of the tied players, their vote decides it |
| **Crown** | once a game, twists on | won in a Trial late in the game: Sanctuary that night, and once, at a Reckoning, its holder may **lay down the Crown** to be safe from that exile |
| **Confessor** (never named in public) | once, in the final, twists on | a special prize for the winner of a Trial in the final: in secret, on their phone, they learn one name that is Forsworn (or that no other Forsworn is left, if the winner is the last one) |

The Whisper, the Last Offer and Errands are Classic only (section 10).

## 6. The end game

- **The final always aims to start with five.** The game sizes every earlier round to land there.
- **With five or fewer**, exiles are no longer revealed.
- **Trust or Doubt:** each player votes on their phone, in secret: TRUST (every Forsworn is gone) or DOUBT (one’s still here). Unanimous TRUST ends the game. Anything else: back to the Reckoning for another unrevealed exile, then vote again.
- **The prize:** the host sets it before the game (the default is **a box of chocolates**). Optionally, **better prizes for a fuller Purse**: one at a half-full Purse, one at a full Purse. If the Forsworn win it works the other way round: half empty wins them the better prize, empty the best. Nobody sees them until the end.
- **The Unmasking:** seals turn one by one.
  - **Only Loyal left:** the Loyal win and share the Purse they built.
  - **A Forsworn left:** the Forsworn win, and take what the Purse fell short.
  - **Two or more Forsworn left: Honour or Betray**, on their phones, in secret. All honour: they split it. One betrays: they take everything. Two or more betray: nobody wins it.
- **The roll:** every player’s true role.

## 7. Awards (up to six, shown at the very end, one at a time)

Modern hands them out from the votes, the Trials and the night: The Sharpest Eye (voted for a Forsworn most) · The Grand Deceiver (the Forsworn who drew the fewest votes all night) · Trial Machine (most Trials won) · The Lightning Rod (most votes against) · First Blood (the first to die) · The Ghost Who Saw (called the most exiles from beyond the table) · Barking Up the Wrong Tree (voted for the Loyal most).

Classic draws from the full list: The Sharpest Eye · The Grand Deceiver · The Deciding Vote · The Turncoat (converted) · Couldn’t Be Bought (refused the Forsworn) · The Ghost Who Saw · The Confessor · The Backstabber (a Forsworn who chalked a fellow Forsworn) · The Narrow Escape (Sanctuary saved them) · First Blood · The Martyr (an exiled Loyal) · Trial Machine · The Kingmaker · The Lightning Rod · The Wild Card (most wrong shouts) · Barking Up the Wrong Tree · Sanctuary Seeker · The Hoarder · The Lone Wolf · The Untouchable · Last Oath Standing.

## 8. The Trials

Fifteen, all played on phones in Modern (details in `trials-modern.csv`): Most Likely To (no prize) · Rapid Quiz · Longest Word · Closest Guess · Higher or Lower · Memory Wall · What Changed? · Snap · Rock Paper Scissors Royale · One Leg · The Statue (needs Ghosts) · Screen Charades · Hum It · Pictionary · Two Truths and a Lie. Plus the Temptation, once a game.

The question banks hold about 3,700 questions and prompts (`question-banks.md`), and nothing repeats within a game.

## 9. How it works (the product)

- **No app, no accounts, no downloads.** Web pages: one on each guest’s phone, one on the TV if you want it.
- **A Table, £15 (Book a Table).** One party. The buyer gets a **booking code** (OATH-XXXX-XXXX; older CNDL- codes still work). It opens one Table for one party of 8 to 24, open for 48 hours from first use. The host chooses Modern or Classic when they open it.
- **A Regular, £20 a month (Become a Regular).** A Regular’s booking code opens a new Table whenever they like, one at a time. Cancel any time.
- **Joining (Modern):** guests scan the QR code on the TV, or go to **thebetrayal.co.uk/join** and type the **Table code** (six letters and numbers) and their name.
- **Resilient:** the game lives on the server, so a phone that drops picks up where it was. A phone that loses its place asks to be let back in, and the host lets them in.
- **The TV never knows a secret**, and no phone is sent more than its player may see (the host’s included).
- **Report a problem** sends us a snapshot and the game’s history.

## 10. Classic: what’s different

**Classic is the secondary option, for anyone who’d prefer it the old-fashioned way.** One phone runs the game, a Keeper reads the script and doesn’t play, guests wear blindfolds at night and chalk their votes on small boards, and the TV puts on the show. Guests don’t need phones. The rules are the same game; these are the differences.

### 10.1 Setup and the Keeper
- **Setup (the Keeper, before guests arrive):** five questions (who’s playing, how long, murder or poison, the prize, who’s keeping) and a Keeper PIN, then a summary that’s ready to start. Every other choice is pre-set and can be changed under **Customise** (Forsworn count, rounds, Trials each round, dinner, the Interval, the narrator, Ghosts, which Trials, Sanctuaries, every twist, the Purse, a title for the night). Details in `setup-guide.md`.
- **The Keeper** runs it like a presenter, knows everything and doesn’t play. If the host wants to play, they choose **“A friend will keep, and I’ll play”**, add themselves to the players and hand the phone to a friend, who presses **Start**, so the host never sees the roles.
- **Props:** a TV with a laptop to cast to it, the Keeper’s phone, and a blindfold, a small chalkboard and chalk per guest.
- **The first game:** the twists are off (the Casting Vote, the Crown, the Temptation, the Confessor, the Whisper, the Last Offer and Errands) so everyone learns the game itself. Once a game has been played on that phone, setup recommends them: “your second night”.
- **The Short game:** about 2 hours for 8 to 10 players: one Trial a round, no Interval, no dinner break.

### 10.2 The evening, Classic style
- **Welcome.** The TV plays the titles and lights a candle for every guest. The Keeper reads the rules while the TV shows them; with the narrator on, the TV reads them aloud.
- **The Oath.** Every guest, blindfolded, swears the oath of loyalty to the table. The game has already chosen the Forsworn at random; as the oath is sworn, the Keeper walks behind the chairs and taps each of them on the shoulder. They take off their blindfolds and see each other.
- **Trials.** The Keeper’s phone shows the next Trial in a pre-dealt running order (the Keeper can swap it) and gives the brief to read; the TV shows the rules, the prize and the game. Answers are shouted or chalked. Wrong shouts in Rapid Quiz, Snap and What Changed? cost the Purse 50 (by default only a Forsworn’s). Some rounds have two Trials.
- **Hidden Sanctuary.** In Trials played in rounds, one secret round can hide Sanctuary; its winner is tapped on the shoulder later, under blindfold. At most a quarter of the table can hold one at once.
- **The Reckoning.** Every player chalks one name. The boards turn **one at a time, in seat order**; the Keeper taps each vote in and the TV counts it live. The Ghosts chalk their call too. A newly dead Loyal gives a secret **verdict** on the Keeper’s phone, naming the player they’re sure is Forsworn.
- **The night.** Blindfolds on. The Keeper wakes the Forsworn by tapping them; they choose in silence. Poison: the Keeper quietly tells the victim, who announces it at dawn with 30 seconds of last words. Murder: the victim is tapped and led away under blindfold.
- **The final.** A last Trial with five; if Sanctuary kept an extra player alive, the table first votes (no reveals) down to five. One more unrevealed vote to four, then Trust or Doubt with the Keeper’s phone passed round. At two left it goes straight to the Unmasking, the Keeper holding a button for the drumroll. Surviving Loyal play rock, paper, scissors for the prize. If two or more Forsworn betray at Honour or Betray, the prize passes to the Ghosts who named a Forsworn (who face the same question), or to nobody.
- **The prize:** up to three, set at setup: the prize, a better one at half the Purse target, the best when it’s full.

### 10.3 Classic’s powers and twists
- **Casting Vote:** once a game, from about halfway; its holder breaks a tie. Won openly or in a hidden round.
- **Crown:** later in the game, Sanctuary and the Casting Vote together; before the first board turns, its holder may lay both down to be immune from that vote (and unprotected that night, without the Casting Vote).
- **Temptation:** offered after a Trial when nobody holds Sanctuary; PURSE or SELF chalked face down.
- **Confessor:** only if exactly one Forsworn is among the last five. The Trial with five left offers “a special prize”; while everyone else is blindfolded, the winner and the last Forsworn look at each other for 30 seconds.
- **Whisper:** when a single Forsworn faces four or more Loyal, that night they whisper to one Loyal instead of killing: an offer to join. Accept, and they become Forsworn; refuse, and the Forsworn knows. The table hears only “nobody died”.
- **Last Offer:** join or die. If a Forsworn is alone again, the chosen Loyal becomes a Forsworn or dies on the spot (Sanctuary still saves them).
- **Errands:** each night the Forsworn get a small secret task for the next Interval (“Get Annie to say the word ‘castle’”). Done: it counts for them at the end. Failed: the Purse loses 50.

### 10.4 How Classic works
- A web page on a laptop connected to the TV (`/t/CODE`) and a web page on the Keeper’s phone (`/t/CODE#keeper`). Guests need nothing.
- Both screens reconnect on their own; the Keeper’s phone keeps every change until the server has it. If it dies, another phone opens the Keeper screen with the Keeper PIN.
- The TV never knows a secret (roles, the PIN and hidden prizes stay on the phone and server).

## 11. Things to never do in marketing

- Never lead with Classic. Modern (a phone each, no props, the host plays too) is the headline; Classic is “prefer it the old-fashioned way?”.
- Never call the Modern host a Keeper.
- Never name, picture or allude to any TV programme, presenter, catchphrase or set. The Betrayal is independent (see `glossary-and-naming.md` for words to avoid).
- Never use an in-game word in public: they are “twists the game explains when they happen”. Never reveal the Confessor or its conditions.
- Never call the product an Invitation or a Standing Invitation, or the opening the Sacrament.
- Don’t say “always four hours”, and don’t sell size or length as a benefit (“up to 24 players, one very long night”). Length depends on the table (section 2).
