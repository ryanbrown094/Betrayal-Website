# The Betrayal: the complete game (internal)

> **Internal: this document contains every secret in the game.** It's for you and the people making marketing with you. Anything customer-facing should follow the public rules (`03-copy/website-copy.md` → "How to play"), which deliberately keep one twist hidden: the Confessor is only ever called "a secret role" or "a special prize".

Everything here matches The Betrayal 1.3 as it plays today. The numbers per table size are in `table-sizes.csv`, the Trials in `missions.csv`, the question banks in `question-banks.md`, and every Keeper setting in `setup-guide.md`.

---

## 1. The idea in one paragraph

A dinner-party game for 8 to 24 guests plus a Keeper. A few guests are secretly **Forsworn**, the killers; everyone else is a **Loyal**. Each round the table plays a party game (a **Trial**) for a shared Purse and for protection, then votes someone out at **the Reckoning**. Each night the Forsworn kill. The Loyal win if every Forsworn is gone at the end; the Forsworn win if even one is still at the table. Two screens run it: the **TV** for the guests, and the **Keeper's phone**, which knows every secret and gives the Keeper every line to read. Guests don't need phones. The props are a blindfold and a small chalkboard each.

## 2. The shape of an evening

1. **Setup (Keeper, about 5 minutes, before guests arrive).** One question a page on the phone, each explained, each with a recommended answer. Or: answer five questions and take the recommendations for the rest.
2. **Welcome.** The TV plays the titles and lights a candle for every guest. The Keeper reads the rules while the TV shows them.
3. **The Forsworn are chosen** (at random, by the game) while everyone is blindfolded. The Keeper taps each Forsworn on the shoulder; they take off their blindfolds and see each other for the first time.
4. **Rounds**, each: Trial(s) → prizes → (Interval) → the Reckoning → reveal → night → dawn. Dinner falls after one round's night.
5. **The final**, with five players: a last Trial, a last vote (no reveal) down to four, then **Trust or Doubt**.
6. **The ceremony**: every seal turned, the winners, the prize, the Purse, then **honour or betray** if two or more Forsworn survived.
7. **Awards.**

Typical length: about 3¼ hours for 8 players, 3½ for 10, 4¼ for 12, 4¾ for 16, 6¼ for 20 to 24 (with an Interval each round and a 25-minute dinner). Bigger tables need more rounds to get down to five.

## 3. Who's who

| role | who | knows | wins when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Loyal** | most guests | only their own role | every Forsworn is gone at the end |
| **Forsworn** | about one in four or five, chosen at random (2 for 8–10 players, 3 for 11–15, 4 for 16–22, 5 for 23–24; the Keeper can change it) | each other | at least one Forsworn is still at the table at the end |
| **Ghost** | anyone exiled or killed, if Ghosts are on | what they see; can't vote or speak at the table | a Loyal Ghost who named a Forsworn can inherit the prize if the surviving Forsworn turn on each other |
| **Keeper** | the person running it | everything | doesn't play |

Nobody is told how many Forsworn there are.

## 4. A round, step by step

### 4.1 The Trial
- The phone shows the next Trial in a pre-dealt running order (Most Likely To first, blindfold games later when Ghosts exist, no repeats until the pool is used). The Keeper can swap it.
- The Keeper reads the brief; the TV shows the rules, then **the prize**, then the game.
- **Points** go into the Purse: each Trial offers a share of what's left to the Purse target, so the Purse fills steadily over the evening.
- **Wrong shouts** (Rapid Quiz, Snap, What Changed?) cost the Purse 50 each. By default only a Forsworn's wrong shout costs anything, so a shrinking Purse is a clue. The room hears the total, never whose.
- Some rounds have two Trials (fixed at setup, or "by the clock": a second one when the evening is running ahead of time).

### 4.2 Prizes: Sanctuary (every round)
- A **Sanctuary** protects its holder from that night's kill. It lasts one night.
- At most a quarter of the table can hold one at once. No Sanctuaries in the final (no night left).
- Won **publicly** (the overall winner, named on the TV) or **hidden**: in Trials played in rounds (quiz, Longest Word, Closest Guess, Higher or Lower, What Changed?), one secret round hides Sanctuary. Whoever wins *that round* takes it, not the Trial winner. They're tapped on the shoulder later, under blindfold. Nobody else knows.
- If a Forsworn wins a hidden Sanctuary it's simply wasted (Forsworn can't be killed), and nobody is told.

### 4.3 The Interval and dinner
- **Interval** (optional, on by default): ten minutes up from the table before each Reckoning. Private chats, deals, accusations. The Forsworn's Errands happen here.
- **Dinner** (optional): a 25-minute break after one round's night, placed mid-game by default. The Keeper can start it later if the food isn't ready.

### 4.4 The Reckoning
- A few minutes of open debate (the TV shows a talk timer).
- Every player chalks one name on their board. The boards turn **one at a time, in seat order**; the Keeper taps each vote in and the TV counts it up live.
- Most votes is **exiled**. A tie goes to a revote between the tied players.
- **More than sixteen at the table:** two exiles, one after the other (the second vote without the Casting Vote).
- **Reveal:** the exiled player's role is shown, Forsworn or Loyal.
- If Ghosts are on, a newly dead Loyal later gives their **verdict**: in secret, on the phone, they name the player they're sure is a Forsworn. Once. It locks.

### 4.5 The night
- Blindfolds on. The Keeper wakes the Forsworn by tapping them; they choose in silence.
- **Victims per night** grow with the table so the final still starts with five: 1 below 12 alive, up to 2 from 12, up to 3 from 20. The game sizes every round to fit the number of rounds the Keeper chose.
- **Poison** (default): the Keeper quietly tells the victim, who stands at dawn, announces it, and gets 30 seconds of last words. **Murder**: the victim is tapped and led away under blindfold; the table wakes to an empty chair.
- Sanctuary saves its holder: "nobody died", and the table doesn't learn why.
- Five left after the Reckoning: **no night**. The final round starts.

### 4.6 Dawn
Blindfolds off, the TV reveals the night, one more candle goes out.

## 5. Powers: Sanctuary every round, everything else once a game

| power | when | what it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Sanctuary** | every round, from Trials | safe from that night's kill |
| **Casting Vote** | once a game, from about halfway (e.g. round 2 of 5) | its holder's vote counts twice at one Reckoning. Won openly or in a hidden round; a hidden Casting Vote's extra vote is shown landing, never whose it was. On the Casting Vote's round, a Trial without rounds offers the Casting Vote *instead of* Sanctuary |
| **Crown** | once a game, later (e.g. round 3 of 5) | Sanctuary and the Casting Vote together. Before the first board turns, the Crown may **lay both down**: nobody may vote for them at that Reckoning, but they lose Sanctuary for the night and vote once |
| **Temptation** | once a game, offered after a Trial when nobody holds Sanctuary | everyone chalks PURSE or SANCTUARY face down. Exactly one SANCTUARY: they take it and the Purse grows. Nobody: the Purse grows by double. Two or more: nobody gets it and the Purse shrinks |
| **Confessor** (secret: "a special prize") | only in the final, only if exactly **one Forsworn** is among the last **five** | the Trial played with five left offers "a special prize". While everyone else is blindfolded, the winner and the last Forsworn take off their blindfolds and look at each other for 30 seconds. A Loyal player Confessor now knows the Forsworn and can't be harmed by them; if the Forsworn wins it, the ceremony plays for show and they meet nobody |

An unclaimed Casting Vote or Crown comes round again on the next Trial. The Keeper can switch any of these off at setup.

## 6. Twists (once a game each)

- **Whisper:** when a single Forsworn faces four or more Loyal players, that night the Forsworn recruits instead of killing. The chosen Loyal accepts (becomes a Forsworn, meets the other) or refuses (stays Loyal; the Forsworn knows). The table only hears "nobody died".
- **Last Offer:** join or die. On a later night, if a Forsworn is alone again (usually because the Whisper was refused), the chosen Loyal becomes a Forsworn or dies on the spot (Sanctuary still saves them).
- **Errands:** each night the Forsworn get a small secret task for the next break ("Get Annie to say the word 'castle'"). Done: it counts for them at the end. Failed: the Purse loses 50.

## 7. The end game

- **The final always aims to start with five.** The game sizes every earlier round to land there (the "unannounced exit" from five to four happens in the final itself).
- **A last Trial** with five at the table, for the Purse (and the Confessor, if it's due).
- If Sanctuary kept an extra player alive and the final opens with six or seven, the table first votes (no reveals) down to five, then plays that Trial.
- **The last Reckoning:** one more vote; the role is **not** revealed. Four left.
- **Trust or Doubt:** the phone goes round; each player votes TRUST (every Forsworn is gone) or DOUBT (one's still here), in secret. Unanimous TRUST ends the game. Anything else: another unrevealed exile, then vote again. At two left it goes straight to the ceremony.
- **The ceremony:** seals turn one by one (the Keeper holds a button for the drumroll).
  - **Only Loyal left:** the Loyal win, split the Purse, and play rock, paper, scissors for the prize if there are two or more.
  - **A Forsworn left:** the Forsworn win.
  - **Two or more Forsworn left: honour or betray.** Each chooses in secret. All share: split the Purse, rock-paper-scissors for the prize. One takes: they take everything. Two or more take: it passes to the Ghosts who named a Forsworn (who then face the same question), or to nobody.
- **The roll:** every player's true role on the TV, converted Forsworn marked.

## 8. Awards (shown at the very end, one at a time)

The Sharpest Eye (chalked a Forsworn most) · The Grand Deceiver (a Forsworn who fooled the table longest / reached the end with fewest votes) · The Deciding Vote · The Turncoat (converted) · Couldn't Be Bought (refused the Forsworn) · The Ghost Who Saw (named a Forsworn from beyond the table) · The Confessor (the Confessor) · The Backstabber (a Forsworn who chalked a fellow Forsworn) · The Narrow Escape (Sanctuary saved them) · First Blood (the first victim) · The Martyr (an exiled Loyal) · Trial Machine (most Trials won) · The Kingmaker · The Lightning Rod (most votes against) · The Wild Card (most wrong shouts) · Barking Up the Wrong Tree (chalked most innocents) · Sanctuary Seeker · The Hoarder · The Lone Wolf · The Untouchable · Last Oath Standing.

## 9. The Trials

Fifteen, plus the Temptation add-on (details in `missions.csv`): Most Likely To (the icebreaker, always first, no prize) · Rapid Quiz · Longest Word · Closest Guess · Memory Wall · Snap · Rock Paper Scissors Royale · One Leg · Screen Charades · Hum It · The Statue (needs Ghosts) · Two Truths and a Lie · Chalkboard Pictionary · Higher or Lower · What Changed?

Hosts can switch any Trial off (e.g. "only games played at the table"). The question banks hold about 3,700 questions and prompts (`question-banks.md`).

## 10. How it works (the product)

- **No app, no accounts, no downloads.** A web page on a laptop connected to the TV, and a web page on the Keeper's phone. Guests need nothing.
- **Buying:** a **Invitation** (£15, one game) or the **monthly subscription** (£20 a month, unlimited games, one at a time; cancel any time). Entering the code opens a room for 48 hours.
- **Resilient:** both screens reconnect on their own; the phone keeps every change until the server has it. If the Keeper's phone dies, another phone opens the Keeper screen with the Keeper PIN.
- **The TV never knows a secret** (roles, the PIN and hidden prizes stay on the phone and server).
- **Report a problem** on either screen sends us a snapshot and the game's history.

## 11. Things to never do in marketing

- Never name, picture or allude to any TV programme, presenter, catchphrase or set. The Betrayal is independent (see `glossary-and-naming.md` for words to avoid).
- Never reveal the Confessor or its conditions in public material.
- Don't say "always four hours": length depends on the table (section 2).
