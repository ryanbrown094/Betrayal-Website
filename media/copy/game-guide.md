# The Betrayal: the complete game (internal)

> **Internal: this document contains every secret in the game.** It’s for you and the people making marketing with you. Anything customer-facing uses only the public words (the Forsworn, the Loyal, the Keeper, the Oath, Trial, the Reckoning, Sanctuary, the Purse, Ghost, the Unmasking, plus plain words like exile, night, dawn, dinner and the Interval). Everything else here (the Casting Vote, the Crown, the Temptation, the Whisper, the Last Offer, Errands, the Confessor, Trust or Doubt, Honour or Betray) is in-game vocabulary: in public it’s one of the “twists the TV explains when they happen”, and the Confessor is never named or hinted at. The two lists are in `glossary-and-naming.md`.

This guide follows the decisions of 29 September 2026 (the Table and the Regular, the Oath, the Casting Vote as a tie-break, PURSE or SELF, the Short game, dinner in courses). The numbers per table size are in `table-sizes.csv`, the Trials in `trials.csv`, the question banks in `question-banks.md`, and every Keeper setting in `setup-guide.md`.

## 1. The idea in one paragraph

A dinner-party game for 8 to 24 guests (best with 8 to 16) plus a Keeper. A few guests are secretly **Forsworn**, the killers; everyone else is **Loyal**. Each round the table plays a party game (a **Trial**) for a shared **Purse** and for protection, then votes someone out at **the Reckoning**. Each night the Forsworn kill. The Loyal win if every Forsworn is gone at the end; the Forsworn win if even one is still at the table. Two screens run it: the **TV** for the guests, and the **Keeper’s phone**, which knows every secret and gives the Keeper every line to read (or has the TV read them aloud). Guests don’t need phones. The props are a blindfold and a small chalkboard each.

## 2. The shape of an evening

1. **Setup (Keeper, before guests arrive).** Five questions (who’s playing, how long, murder or poison, the prize, who’s keeping) and a Keeper PIN, then a summary that’s ready to start. Every other choice is pre-set from those answers and can be changed under **Customise**. Details in `setup-guide.md`.
2. **Welcome.** The TV plays the titles and lights a candle for every guest. The Keeper reads the rules while the TV shows them; with the narrator on, the TV reads them aloud.
3. **The Oath.** Every guest, blindfolded, swears the oath of loyalty to the table. The game has already chosen the Forsworn at random; as the oath is sworn, the Keeper walks behind the chairs and taps each of them on the shoulder. They take off their blindfolds and see each other for the first time.
4. **Rounds**, each: Trial(s) → prizes → (Interval) → the Reckoning → reveal → night → dawn. Dinner can come in three courses between the rounds, or as one break (4.3).
5. **The final**, with five players: a last Trial, a last vote (no reveal) down to four, then **Trust or Doubt**.
6. **The Unmasking**: every seal turned, the winners, the prize the Purse has earned, then **Honour or Betray** if two or more Forsworn survived.
7. **Awards**, up to six.

**How long:** 8 to 12 players, about 3 to 4 hours with dinner. The **Short game**, about 2 hours for 8 to 10 players: one Trial a round, no Interval, no dinner break. Bigger tables run longer, because they need more rounds to get down to five: 20 or more, about 6 hours. Setup estimates the length for each table, and `table-sizes.csv` lists every size. In marketing, state the length honestly and never sell a big table or a long night as a benefit.

### The first game: Classic

The first game played on a phone is set up as **Classic**: Sanctuary won openly, Ghosts, an Interval and dinner, with the twists off (the Casting Vote, the Crown, the Temptation, the Confessor, the Whisper, the Last Offer and Errands). Once a game has been played on that phone, setup recommends the twists: “your second night”. Either way, everything can be changed under Customise.

### When the host wants to play

At “Who’s keeping?”, the host chooses **“A friend will keep, and I’ll play”**. They add themselves to the players and hand the phone to a friend, who presses **Start**, so the host never sees the roles. The friend is the Keeper from then on.

## 3. Who’s who

| role | who | knows | wins when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Loyal** | most guests | only their own role | every Forsworn is gone at the end |
| **Forsworn** | about one in four or five, chosen at random (2 for 8–10 players, 3 for 11–15, 4 for 16–22, 5 for 23–24; the Keeper can change it) | each other | at least one Forsworn is still at the table at the end |
| **Ghost** | anyone exiled or killed, if Ghosts are on | what they see; can’t vote or speak at the table | a Loyal Ghost who named a Forsworn can inherit the prize if the surviving Forsworn turn on each other; the best Ghost can win an award |
| **Keeper** | the host, or a friend keeping so the host can play | everything | doesn’t play |

Nobody is told how many Forsworn there are.

## 4. A round, step by step

### 4.1 The Trial
- The phone shows the next Trial in a pre-dealt running order (Most Likely To first, blindfold games later when Ghosts exist, no repeats until the pool is used). The Keeper can swap it.
- The Keeper reads the brief; the TV shows the rules, then **the prize**, then the game.
- **Points** go into the Purse: each Trial offers a share of what’s left to the Purse target, so the Purse fills steadily over the evening.
- **Wrong shouts** (Rapid Quiz, Snap, What Changed?) cost the Purse 50 each. By default only a Forsworn’s wrong shout costs anything, so a shrinking Purse is a clue. The room hears the total, never whose.
- Some rounds have two Trials (fixed at setup, or “by the clock”: a second one when the evening is running ahead of time). The Short game plays one Trial a round.

### 4.2 Prizes: Sanctuary (every round)
- **Sanctuary** protects its holder from that night’s kill. It lasts one night.
- At most a quarter of the table can hold one at once. No Sanctuaries in the final (no night left).
- Won **openly** (the overall winner, named on the TV) or **hidden**: in Trials played in rounds (quiz, Longest Word, Closest Guess, Higher or Lower, What Changed?), one secret round hides Sanctuary. Whoever wins *that round* takes it, not the Trial winner. They’re tapped on the shoulder later, under blindfold. Nobody else knows. Classic wins every Sanctuary openly.
- If a Forsworn wins a hidden Sanctuary it’s simply wasted (Forsworn can’t be killed), and nobody is told.

### 4.3 The Interval and dinner
- **Interval** (optional, on by default; off in the Short game): ten minutes up from the table before each Reckoning. Private chats, deals, accusations. The Forsworn’s Errands happen here.
- **Dinner, served in three courses** (optional): a starter after round one (15 minutes), the main course mid-game (25), dessert before the final (15). Courses need four or more rounds; otherwise there’s one dinner break, or none. The Short game has no dinner break. The Keeper can start a break later if the food isn’t ready.

### 4.4 The Reckoning
- A few minutes of open debate (the TV shows a talk timer).
- Every player chalks one name on their board. The boards turn **one at a time, in seat order**; the Keeper taps each vote in and the TV counts it up live.
- Most votes is **exiled**. A tie goes to a revote between the tied players, unless the Casting Vote breaks it (section 5).
- **Ghosts’ call:** if Ghosts are on, the Ghosts chalk who they think will be exiled. Those who called it are named on the TV, and the best Ghost can win an award at the end.
- **More than sixteen at the table:** two exiles, one after the other.
- **Reveal:** the exiled player’s role is shown, Forsworn or Loyal.
- If Ghosts are on, a newly dead Loyal later gives their **verdict**: in secret, on the phone, they name the player they’re sure is a Forsworn. Once. It locks.

### 4.5 The night
- Blindfolds on. The Keeper wakes the Forsworn by tapping them; they choose in silence.
- **Victims per night** grow with the table so the final still starts with five: 1 below 12 alive, up to 2 from 12, up to 3 from 20. The game sizes every round to fit the number of rounds.
- **Poison** (default): the Keeper quietly tells the victim, who stands at dawn, announces it, and gets 30 seconds of last words. **Murder**: the victim is tapped and led away under blindfold; the table wakes to an empty chair.
- Sanctuary saves its holder: “nobody died”, and the table doesn’t learn why.
- Five left after the Reckoning: **no night**. The final round starts.

### 4.6 Dawn
Blindfolds off, the TV reveals the night, one more candle goes out.

## 5. Powers: Sanctuary every round, everything else once a game

All of these except Sanctuary are off in Classic (the first game on a phone) and recommended from the second night.

| power | when | what it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Sanctuary** | every round, from Trials | safe from that night’s kill |
| **Casting Vote** | once a game, from about halfway (e.g. round 2 of 5) | its holder **breaks a tie** at one Reckoning: when the count ends level, the Casting Vote decides between the tied players. Won openly or in a hidden round. On the Casting Vote’s round, a Trial without rounds offers the Casting Vote *instead of* Sanctuary |
| **Crown** | once a game, later (e.g. round 3 of 5) | Sanctuary and the Casting Vote together. Before the first board turns, its holder may **lay both down**: nobody may vote for them at that Reckoning, but they are unprotected that night and give up the Casting Vote |
| **Temptation** | once a game, offered after a Trial when nobody holds Sanctuary | everyone chalks **PURSE or SELF** face down. Exactly one SELF: they take Sanctuary and the Purse grows. Nobody: the Purse grows by double. Two or more: nobody gets Sanctuary and the Purse loses points |
| **Confessor** (never named in public) | only in the final, only if exactly **one Forsworn** is among the last **five** | the Trial played with five left offers “a special prize”. While everyone else is blindfolded, the winner and the last Forsworn take off their blindfolds and look at each other for 30 seconds. A Loyal Confessor now knows the Forsworn and can’t be harmed by them; if the Forsworn wins it, the scene plays for show and they meet nobody |

An unclaimed Casting Vote or Crown comes round again on the next Trial. The Keeper can switch any of these off under Customise.

## 6. Twists (once a game each)

- **Whisper:** when a single Forsworn faces four or more Loyal players, that night the Forsworn whispers to one Loyal instead of killing: an offer to join them. The chosen Loyal accepts (becomes a Forsworn, meets the other) or refuses (stays Loyal; the Forsworn knows). The table only hears “nobody died”.
- **Last Offer:** join or die. On a later night, if a Forsworn is alone again (usually because the Whisper was refused), the chosen Loyal becomes a Forsworn or dies on the spot (Sanctuary still saves them).
- **Errands:** each night the Forsworn get a small secret task for the next break (“Get Annie to say the word ‘castle’”). Done: it counts for them at the end. Failed: the Purse loses 50.

## 7. The end game

- **The final always aims to start with five.** The game sizes every earlier round to land there (the unannounced exit from five to four happens in the final itself).
- **A last Trial** with five at the table, for the Purse (and the Confessor, if it’s due).
- If Sanctuary kept an extra player alive and the final opens with six or seven, the table first votes (no reveals) down to five, then plays that Trial.
- **The last Reckoning:** one more vote; the role is **not** revealed. Four left.
- **Trust or Doubt:** the phone goes round; each player votes TRUST (every Forsworn is gone) or DOUBT (one’s still here), in secret. Unanimous TRUST ends the game. Anything else: another unrevealed exile, then vote again. At two left it goes straight to the Unmasking.
- **The prize:** the Keeper can set up to three at setup, and the Purse decides which is won: the prize, a better one if the Purse reaches half its target, the best if it’s full. They stay secret until the end.
- **The Unmasking:** seals turn one by one (the Keeper holds a button for the drumroll).
  - **Only Loyal left:** the Loyal win, split the Purse, and play rock, paper, scissors for the prize if there are two or more.
  - **A Forsworn left:** the Forsworn win.
  - **Two or more Forsworn left: Honour or Betray.** Each chooses in secret. All honour: split the Purse, rock, paper, scissors for the prize. One betrays: they take everything. Two or more betray: it passes to the Ghosts who named a Forsworn (who then face the same question), or to nobody.
- **The roll:** every player’s true role on the TV, converted Forsworn marked.

## 8. Awards (up to six, shown at the very end, one at a time)

The Sharpest Eye (chalked a Forsworn most) · The Grand Deceiver (a Forsworn who fooled the table longest / reached the end with fewest votes) · The Deciding Vote · The Turncoat (converted) · Couldn’t Be Bought (refused the Forsworn) · The Ghost Who Saw (named a Forsworn from beyond the table) · The Confessor (the Confessor) · The Backstabber (a Forsworn who chalked a fellow Forsworn) · The Narrow Escape (Sanctuary saved them) · First Blood (the first victim) · The Martyr (an exiled Loyal) · Trial Machine (most Trials won) · The Kingmaker · The Lightning Rod (most votes against) · The Wild Card (most wrong shouts) · Barking Up the Wrong Tree (chalked most innocents) · Sanctuary Seeker · The Hoarder · The Lone Wolf · The Untouchable · Last Oath Standing.

## 9. The Trials

Fifteen, plus the Temptation add-on (details in `trials.csv`): Most Likely To (the icebreaker, always first, no prize) · Rapid Quiz · Longest Word · Closest Guess · Memory Wall · Snap · Rock Paper Scissors Royale · One Leg · Screen Charades · Hum It · The Statue (needs Ghosts) · Two Truths and a Lie · Chalkboard Pictionary · Higher or Lower · What Changed?

Hosts can switch any Trial off (e.g. “only games played at the table”). The question banks hold about 3,700 questions and prompts (`question-banks.md`).

## 10. How it works (the product)

- **No app, no accounts, no downloads.** A web page on a laptop connected to the TV, and a web page on the Keeper’s phone. Guests need nothing.
- **A Table, £15 (Book a Table).** One party. The buyer gets a **booking code** (OATH-XXXX-XXXX; older CNDL- codes still work). It opens one Table for one party of 8 to 24, open for 48 hours from first use.
- **A Regular, £20 a month (Become a Regular).** A Regular’s booking code opens a new Table whenever they like, one at a time. Cancel any time.
- **The Table code:** the six characters the TV joins with, shown on the Keeper’s phone.
- **The narrator** (optional): the TV reads the Keeper’s lines aloud.
- **Resilient:** both screens reconnect on their own; the phone keeps every change until the server has it. If the Keeper’s phone dies, another phone opens the Keeper screen with the Keeper PIN.
- **The TV never knows a secret** (roles, the PIN and hidden prizes stay on the phone and server).
- **Report a problem** on either screen sends us a snapshot and the game’s history.

## 11. Things to never do in marketing

- Never name, picture or allude to any TV programme, presenter, catchphrase or set. The Betrayal is independent (see `glossary-and-naming.md` for words to avoid).
- Never use an in-game word in public: they are “twists the TV explains when they happen”. Never reveal the Confessor or its conditions.
- Never call the product an Invitation or a Standing Invitation, or the opening the Sacrament.
- Don’t say “always four hours”, and don’t sell size or length as a benefit (“up to 24 players, one very long night”). Length depends on the table (section 2).
