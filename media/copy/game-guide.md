# Cloak & Candle: the complete game (internal)

> **Internal: this document contains every secret in the game.** It's for you and the people making marketing with you. Anything customer-facing should follow the public rules (`03-copy/website-copy.md` → "How to play"), which deliberately keep one twist hidden: the Witness is only ever called "a secret role" or "a special prize".

Everything here matches Cloak & Candle 1.3 as it plays today. The numbers per table size are in `table-sizes.csv`, the missions in `missions.csv`, the question banks in `question-banks.md`, and every host setting in `setup-guide.md`.

---

## 1. The idea in one paragraph

A dinner-party game for 8 to 24 guests plus a host. A few guests are secretly **Cloaks**, the killers; everyone else is a **Candle**. Each round the table plays a party game (a **mission**) for a shared pot and for protection, then votes someone out at **the Reckoning**. Each night the Cloaks kill. The Candles win if every Cloak is gone at the end; the Cloaks win if even one is still at the table. Two screens run it: the **TV** for the guests, and the **host's phone**, which knows every secret and gives the host every line to read. Guests don't need phones. The props are a blindfold and a small chalkboard each.

## 2. The shape of an evening

1. **Setup (host, about 5 minutes, before guests arrive).** One question a page on the phone, each explained, each with a recommended answer. Or: answer five questions and take the recommendations for the rest.
2. **Welcome.** The TV plays the titles and lights a candle for every guest. The host reads the rules while the TV shows them.
3. **The Cloaks are chosen** (at random, by the game) while everyone is blindfolded. The host taps each Cloak on the shoulder; they take off their blindfolds and see each other for the first time.
4. **Rounds**, each: mission(s) → prizes → (mingle) → the Reckoning → reveal → night → dawn. Dinner falls after one round's night.
5. **The final**, with five players: a last mission, a last vote (no reveal) down to four, then **End or Exile**.
6. **The ceremony**: every seal turned, the winners, the prize, the pot, then **share or take** if two or more Cloaks survived.
7. **Awards.**

Typical length: about 3¼ hours for 8 players, 3½ for 10, 4¼ for 12, 4¾ for 16, 6¼ for 20 to 24 (with a mingle each round and a 25-minute dinner). Bigger tables need more rounds to get down to five.

## 3. Who's who

| role | who | knows | wins when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Candle** | most guests | only their own role | every Cloak is gone at the end |
| **Cloak** | about one in four or five, chosen at random (2 for 8–10 players, 3 for 11–15, 4 for 16–22, 5 for 23–24; the host can change it) | each other | at least one Cloak is still at the table at the end |
| **Ghost** | anyone exiled or killed, if Ghosts are on | what they see; can't vote or speak at the table | a Candle Ghost who named a Cloak can inherit the prize if the surviving Cloaks turn on each other |
| **Host** | the person running it | everything | doesn't play |

Nobody is told how many Cloaks there are.

## 4. A round, step by step

### 4.1 The mission
- The phone shows the next mission in a pre-dealt running order (Most Likely To first, blindfold games later when Ghosts exist, no repeats until the pool is used). The host can swap it.
- The host reads the brief; the TV shows the rules, then **the prize**, then the game.
- **Points** go into the pot: each mission offers a share of what's left to the pot target, so the pot fills steadily over the evening.
- **Wrong shouts** (Rapid Quiz, Snap, What Changed?) cost the pot 50 each. By default only a Cloak's wrong shout costs anything, so a shrinking pot is a clue. The room hears the total, never whose.
- Some rounds have two missions (fixed at setup, or "by the clock": a second one when the evening is running ahead of time).

### 4.2 Prizes: the shield (every round)
- A **shield** protects its holder from that night's kill. It lasts one night.
- At most a quarter of the table can hold one at once. No shields in the final (no night left).
- Won **publicly** (the overall winner, named on the TV) or **hidden**: in missions played in rounds (quiz, Longest Word, Closest Guess, Higher or Lower, What Changed?), one secret round hides the shield. Whoever wins *that round* takes it, not the mission winner. They're tapped on the shoulder later, under blindfold. Nobody else knows.
- If a Cloak wins a hidden shield it's simply wasted (Cloaks can't be killed), and nobody is told.

### 4.3 The mingle and dinner
- **Mingle** (optional, on by default): ten minutes up from the table before each Reckoning. Private chats, deals, accusations. The Cloaks' side-quests happen here.
- **Dinner** (optional): a 25-minute break after one round's night, placed mid-game by default. The host can start it later if the food isn't ready.

### 4.4 The Reckoning
- A few minutes of open debate (the TV shows a talk timer).
- Every player chalks one name on their board. The boards turn **one at a time, in seat order**; the host taps each vote in and the TV counts it up live.
- Most votes is **exiled**. A tie goes to a revote between the tied players.
- **More than sixteen at the table:** two exiles, one after the other (the second vote without the blade).
- **Reveal:** the exiled player's role is shown, Cloak or Candle.
- If Ghosts are on, a newly dead Candle later gives their **verdict**: in secret, on the phone, they name the player they're sure is a Cloak. Once. It locks.

### 4.5 The night
- Blindfolds on. The host wakes the Cloaks by tapping them; they choose in silence.
- **Victims per night** grow with the table so the final still starts with five: 1 below 12 alive, up to 2 from 12, up to 3 from 20. The game sizes every round to fit the number of rounds the host chose.
- **Poison** (default): the host quietly tells the victim, who stands at dawn, announces it, and gets 30 seconds of last words. **Murder**: the victim is tapped and led away under blindfold; the table wakes to an empty chair.
- A shield saves its holder: "nobody died", and the table doesn't learn why.
- Five left after the Reckoning: **no night**. The final round starts.

### 4.6 Dawn
Blindfolds off, the TV reveals the night, one more candle goes out.

## 5. Powers: the shield every round, everything else once a game

| power | when | what it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Shield** | every round, from missions | safe from that night's kill |
| **Blade** | once a game, from about halfway (e.g. round 2 of 5) | its holder's vote counts twice at one Reckoning. Won openly or in a hidden round; a hidden blade's extra vote is shown landing, never whose it was. On the blade's round, a mission without rounds offers the blade *instead of* the shield |
| **Champion** | once a game, later (e.g. round 3 of 5) | a shield and the blade together. Before the first board turns, the Champion may **lay both down**: nobody may vote for them at that Reckoning, but they lose the shield for the night and vote once |
| **Dilemma** | once a game, offered after a mission when nobody holds a shield | everyone chalks POT or SHIELD face down. Exactly one SHIELD: they take it and the pot grows. Nobody: the pot grows by double. Two or more: nobody gets it and the pot shrinks |
| **Witness** (secret: "a special prize") | only in the final, only if exactly **one Cloak** is among the last **five** | the mission played with five left offers "a special prize". While everyone else is blindfolded, the winner and the last Cloak take off their blindfolds and look at each other for 30 seconds. A Candle Witness now knows the Cloak and can't be harmed by them; if the Cloak wins it, the ceremony plays for show and they meet nobody |

An unclaimed blade or Champion comes round again on the next mission. The host can switch any of these off at setup.

## 6. Twists (once a game each)

- **Conversion:** when a single Cloak faces four or more Candles, that night the Cloak recruits instead of killing. The chosen Candle accepts (becomes a Cloak, meets the other) or refuses (stays a Candle; the Cloak knows). The table only hears "nobody died".
- **Bargain:** join or die. On a later night, if a Cloak is alone again (usually because the Conversion was refused), the chosen Candle becomes a Cloak or dies on the spot (a shield still saves them).
- **Side-quests:** each night the Cloaks get a small secret task for the next break ("Get Annie to say the word 'castle'"). Done: it counts for them at the end. Failed: the pot loses 50.

## 7. The end game

- **The final always aims to start with five.** The game sizes every earlier round to land there (the "unannounced exit" from five to four happens in the final itself).
- **A last mission** with five at the table, for the pot (and the Witness, if it's due).
- If a shield kept an extra player alive and the final opens with six or seven, the table first votes (no reveals) down to five, then plays that mission.
- **The last Reckoning:** one more vote; the role is **not** revealed. Four left.
- **End or Exile:** the phone goes round; each player votes END (every Cloak is gone) or EXILE (one's still here), in secret. Unanimous END ends the game. Anything else: another unrevealed exile, then vote again. At two left it goes straight to the ceremony.
- **The ceremony:** seals turn one by one (the host holds a button for the drumroll).
  - **Only Candles left:** the Candles win, split the pot, and play rock, paper, scissors for the prize if there are two or more.
  - **A Cloak left:** the Cloaks win.
  - **Two or more Cloaks left: share or take.** Each chooses in secret. All share: split the pot, rock-paper-scissors for the prize. One takes: they take everything. Two or more take: it passes to the Ghosts who named a Cloak (who then face the same question), or to nobody.
- **The roll:** every player's true role on the TV, converted Cloaks marked.

## 8. Awards (shown at the very end, one at a time)

The Sharpest Eye (chalked a Cloak most) · The Grand Deceiver (a Cloak who fooled the table longest / reached the end with fewest votes) · The Deciding Blade · The Turncoat (converted) · Couldn't Be Bought (refused the Cloaks) · The Ghost Who Saw (named a Cloak from beyond the table) · The Prophet (the Witness) · The Backstabber (a Cloak who chalked a fellow Cloak) · The Narrow Escape (a shield saved them) · First Blood (the first victim) · The Martyr (an exiled Candle) · Mission Machine (most missions won) · The Kingmaker · The Lightning Rod (most votes against) · The Wild Card (most wrong shouts) · Barking Up the Wrong Tree (chalked most innocents) · The Shield Bearer · The Point Hoarder · The Lone Wolf · The Untouchable · Last Candle Burning.

## 9. The missions

Fifteen, plus the Dilemma add-on (details in `missions.csv`): Most Likely To (the icebreaker, always first, no prize) · Rapid Quiz · Longest Word · Closest Guess · Memory Wall · Snap · Rock Paper Scissors Royale · One Leg · Screen Charades · Hum It · The Statue (needs Ghosts) · Two Truths and a Lie · Chalkboard Pictionary · Higher or Lower · What Changed?

Hosts can switch any mission off (e.g. "only games played at the table"). The question banks hold about 3,700 questions and prompts (`question-banks.md`).

## 10. How it works (the product)

- **No app, no accounts, no downloads.** A web page on a laptop connected to the TV, and a web page on the host's phone. Guests need nothing.
- **Buying:** a **party pass** (£15, one game) or the **monthly subscription** (£20 a month, unlimited games, one at a time; cancel any time). Entering the code opens a room for 48 hours.
- **Resilient:** both screens reconnect on their own; the phone keeps every change until the server has it. If the host's phone dies, another phone opens the host screen with the host PIN.
- **The TV never knows a secret** (roles, the PIN and hidden prizes stay on the phone and server).
- **Report a problem** on either screen sends us a snapshot and the game's history.

## 11. Things to never do in marketing

- Never name, picture or allude to any TV programme, presenter, catchphrase or set. Cloak & Candle is independent (see `glossary-and-naming.md` for words to avoid).
- Never reveal the Witness or its conditions in public material.
- Don't say "always four hours": length depends on the table (section 2).
