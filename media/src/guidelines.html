01 · the brand

Candlelit. Theatrical. Mischievous.

Cloak & Candle is a hosted party game for one room and one evening. Its world is a long table, a candle burning for every guest, and a cloak that somebody at the table is wearing. Each night a candle goes out; each day the Candles try to pull the cloak away. Everything we make should feel like that room: warm, dim, a little dangerous, and very well dressed.

Candlelit

Dark grounds, warm light. We glow; we never glare. Rose-gold is the candle flame of the brand. The cloak is the dark around it.

Theatrical

Every screen is a scene. Big type, slow reveals, a pause before the name. The host is a narrator, not a quizmaster.

Mischievous

We enjoy the lie. We wink at the guilty and tease the innocent — never mean, never crude.

Premium

One idea per screen, generous space, careful words. It should feel like the good napkins came out.

What we are

  • Our own world. An original game with its own look, sound and words.
  • For hosts. The host is the hero: we make them look effortless.
  • For one room. Faces, blindfolds, chalk — the TV is the stage, not the game.

What we are not

  • Not a TV tie-in. We never name, quote, imitate or allude to a television programme, its presenters, its castle, its music or its logo.
  • Not horror. No gore, no jump-scares. A drop of wine-red, not blood spatter.
  • Not a gadget. No apps to install, no accounts, no guests on their phones.

02 · the mark

The one in the cloak

A cloaked figure stands inside a lozenge, and where its face should be there is only a candle flame. It is the whole game in one picture: someone at the table is hiding under the cloak, and the candle is what gives them away.

Anatomy

  • The lozenge — a double diamond frame, the outer line at full weight, the inner at half. It sets the mark apart from shields and crests.
  • The cloak — a pointed hood falling to a straight hem, with two folds and a clasp at the throat. Never add hands, feet or a face.
  • The flame — a filled teardrop inside the hood’s opening. It is the only solid shape in the mark.
  • The pins — two short rails and studs at the side points, like a brooch or a seal.

The wordmark

“Cloak & Candle” set in Grenze Gotisch SemiBold (600) and converted to outlines. The ampersand is part of the name. Always use the supplied artwork; never retype it.

04 · space, size, misuse

Give it room to breathe

Clear space

Keep a margin of at least X on every side, where X is the height of the flame — about one sixth of the mark’s height. Nothing — type, edges, other logos — enters it.

Minimum sizes

  • Mark: 8 mm / 24 px tall. Smaller than that, the fine inner line fills in.
  • Horizontal lockup: 30 mm / 120 px wide.
  • Stacked lockup: 22 mm / 90 px wide.

Files

Every lockup and colourway is supplied as SVG (outlined, no fonts needed) and as transparent PNG at 2400 px in media-pack/logos.

Never

Stretch or squash.
Turn it gold, or any other colour.
Add shadows, bevels or outlines.
Set it on a clashing or busy ground.
Rotate it.
Cloak and Candle
Retype it, swap the ampersand for “and”, or pair it with a show’s name or logo.

05 · colour

Plum ink and rose-gold

06 · typography

Four voices, one table

The Reckoning
Grenze Gotisch · SemiBold 600 · display: titles, names, the big reveal
Accuse. Defend. Deflect. When the talking stops, the chalk comes out.
IM Fell DW Pica · Regular & Italic · narrative: the lines the room reads
Round two · mission one · the prize
Alegreya Sans SC · Bold 700, tracked +300 · labels, eyebrows, name plaques
Everything a host needs to read at a glance: settings, notes, the private context, buttons. Clear before clever.
Alegreya Sans · 400 / 500 / 700 · interface and body copy

Rules

  • One display line per screen. Grenze Gotisch is for headlines and names — never a paragraph, never all caps.
  • Blackletter needs space. At least 1.5× its cap height above and below; minimum 18 pt in print, 32 px on screen.
  • Italic tells the story. IM Fell Italic is the narrator: short, spoken lines.
  • Caps are always tracked. Alegreya Sans SC, +200 to +400.
  • On the host phone use the device’s system font for speed and legibility; the brand lives on the TV.

Licences

All four families are released under the SIL Open Font License 1.1 — free for commercial use, embedding and print. Keep a copy of the licence with any font files you distribute.

07 · the world

Candles, cloaks and seals

Iconography

Line icons on a 64-unit grid, 2.4 stroke, round caps and joins, one solid detail at most. Shield, blade, chalice, moon, eye, ghost, candle, hourglass: the objects of the evening.

Motifs

  • Wax seals stand for people: one colour each, initial pressed in blackletter. A Cloak’s seal cracks.
  • Candles are the guests: one each, lit as they sit down. A snuffed candle is a death — the only place we show one.
  • The cloak is the Cloaks’: never drawn as a person with a face — a hood, a fold, a shadow at the edge of the light.
  • Lancet windows frame the TV; their glass changes with the hour (dawn rose, day amber, night steel).
  • Mist and dust — slow and low. Nothing flashes except a reveal.

All artwork is original, drawn in code. Use real game screens rather than mock-ups wherever the product is shown.

08 · voice

The narrator at the head of the table

We speak like a host who has been waiting all evening to say this: second person, present tense, a little wicked, never wordy. Short sentences. A pause before the name.

Sounds like us

“Blindfolds on. Hands flat on the table. If I hear a single word, you will answer for it at dawn.”
“If you feel my hand on your shoulder, you take the cloak.”
“Look at the screen: there is a candle burning for every one of you.”
“Every cloak has been pulled away. The Candles have won.”

Do

  • Address the room directly: you, your table, tonight.
  • Use British English and plain words: chalk a name, not submit a vote.
  • Keep secrets in the copy too — never hint who holds a role.
  • Call the prize the prize until it is won.

Don’t

  • Name, quote or allude to any TV programme, presenter, catchphrase, castle or theme tune.
  • Write gore or cruelty. Murder is a tap on the shoulder, not a crime scene.
  • Use tech words in front of guests: app, sync, user, click.
  • Pile on adjectives. One good word, then stop.

Naming

Game words are capitalised: Cloak, Candle, Ghost, the Reckoning, the Champion, the Witness, the Dilemma. Everything else is lower case: shield, blade, pot, mission, night, dawn.
